- exact user quotes about the chapter;
- story-time chronology;
- canon claims;
- character/world states;
- scene structure;
- chapter intent;
- hook;
- previous-chapter effect;
- next-chapter effect;
- prose mechanics;
- typography;
- cadence;
- tokenization/jaggedness;
- lexical density;
- readability;
- delivery functions;
- rejected/noncommitted variants;
- contradictions;
- unknowns;

## No premature canonization
A Legacy chapter can establish evidence about what the manuscript contains without automatically establishing that every statement inside it is current world canon.

Manuscript text, author commentary, planning notes, and assistant reconstruction remain distinct evidence classes.

## Variant rule
If multiple Chapter N artifacts exist:
- preserve every materially different variant;
- identify exact differences;
- determine provenance and chronology;
- do not choose a "final" version by filename alone;
- require explicit release evidence or author confirmation for current/released status.

## Prose measurement rule
All numerical/textual prose measurements must be calculated from the actual received manuscript. Never estimate them from memory.

## Mental-model rule
Each recovered concept receives G1/G2/G3 independently of canon status.

## Contradiction rule
Conflicts between Legacy Chapters and later material are recorded, not silently repaired. A later explicit author correction may supersede an earlier state, but the earlier state remains historical evidence.

## Hard STOP conditions
STOP rather than fabricate when:
- the received text is incomplete;
- chapter identity is ambiguous;
- two variants cannot be ordered;
- a quotation cannot be verified;
- a supposed "final" status lacks evidence;
- a requested prose metric cannot be calculated from the supplied text.
