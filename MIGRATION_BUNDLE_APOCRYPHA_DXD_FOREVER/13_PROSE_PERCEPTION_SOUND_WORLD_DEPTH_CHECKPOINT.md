## 4. Perceptual completeness and visual coherence

### Working mechanism

A useful working model is: **stimulus → automatic/pre-attentive registration → perceptual organization → bodily/affective response → conscious interpretation (if any) → association or thought (if any) → evaluation → action → changed environment → new stimulus**.

This is an explanatory working model, not a rigid serial pipeline. Processes can overlap, recur, or never become conscious.

### Observable prose effects

- Ira can register something before he knows what it means.
- Attention can shift without an explicit decision.
- A bodily reaction can precede an explanation.
- Thought may be goal-directed, associative, trivial, self-serving, mistaken, fragmented, or absent.
- Perception is selective; narration need not report every stimulus.
- The physical scene can be clear while Ira's interpretation remains uncertain.

### Visual dissonance and perceptual anchoring

**Visual clarity is not the same as visual inventory.** A paragraph can name many objects and still produce an unstable mental image if it does not establish their relationships.

Implementation:
1. Establish a base anchor: Ira's position, a stable landmark, a boundary, or direction of travel.
2. Attach new spatial information to that anchor.
3. Show movement relative to known geometry.
4. Keep camera height, facing direction, distance, and line of sight stable unless the text motivates a change.
5. Separate what Ira sees from what he infers.
6. If several incompatible visual interpretations are possible, clarify physical arrangement before adding more detail.

Track perceptual distance as separate variables: physical distance; visual access/occlusion; social distance; emotional distance; cognitive access; narrative distance. A person may be physically close but socially remote, or distant yet perceptually dominant.

### Failure conditions and test

Failures: object lists without a stable scene; incompatible reader interpretations of vehicle/room/street geometry; unmotivated overhead-camera drift; polished conclusions the character has not plausibly reached; or constant explicit internal monologue that makes cognition artificial.

Test: ask a reader to sketch positions, movement, sightlines, and boundaries, then identify what Ira noticed first, inferred, and could not know. If physical layouts diverge, fix anchoring before adding sensory detail.

## 5. Sound model — provisional research framework

**Sound in prose is a changing information field with sources, positions, distance, interference, attention, interpretation, and consequences.**

Five layers:
- **S1 — Acoustic world:** voices, bodies, machines, animals, weather, infrastructure, architecture, and possible supernatural sources.
- **S2 — Acoustic geography:** direction, distance, elevation, enclosure, reverberation, obstruction, movement, and source visibility.
- **S3 — Acoustic cognition:** automatic registration, foreground/background, attention, masking, recognition, uncertainty, prediction, and bodily response.
- **S4 — Narrative sound:** location, threat, social activity, history, technology, institutional operation, unseen events, atmosphere, and world scale.
- **S5 — Textual sound:** sentence rhythm, stress, syntactic grouping, punctuation, pauses, repetition, phonological texture, dialogue rhythm, paragraph tempo, and silence.

### Mechanism → effect → prose translation

- **Auditory grouping and attention:** multiple sources are organized into streams; attention can promote a source without erasing every other source. Establish a baseline, then let relevant sound emerge.
- **Masking/interference:** one source can obscure another. Make speech or cues genuinely difficult to hear when the environment warrants it.
- **Localization:** direction and distance are inferred from cues including level, reverberation, obstruction, and movement. Establish where a sound comes from, how far away it is, what lies between it and Ira, and what changes as he moves.
- **Reverberation:** acoustic response can imply enclosure, surfaces, and spatial scale. Use it to clarify architecture, not as a technical inventory.
- **Off-screen sound:** sound can reveal movement or activity outside visual access and change expectation, attention, or action.
- **Changing baseline:** a familiar sound stopping can make its absence perceptible. Silence often works best as a change in a known acoustic field.
- **Movement through spaces:** doors, corridors, platforms, vehicles, tunnels, and open air change what is audible and how it sounds.
- **Textual prosody:** readers construct implicit rhythm, grouping, stress, and voice while reading silently. Syntax and punctuation shape experience; rhythm is more than alternating short and long sentences.
- **Sound as social infrastructure:** who hears, who is masked, who overhears, and what fails to circulate can reveal privacy, institutions, technology, and power.

A reusable diagnostic sequence—not a mandatory formula—is **baseline → intrusion → competition/masking → attention shift → dominance or recognition → disappearance/residue → changed baseline**.

Do not force every scene into this sequence. **When sound matters, make it causally real.** Do not stuff every paragraph with sound effects or aim for ‘Dolby Atmos prose’ everywhere.

### Sound failure conditions and test

Failures: ‘the station was loud’ without acoustic geography or consequence; sound-effect lists without source positions or attention hierarchy; perfectly intelligible dialogue through implausible noise; sound used only as mood decoration; phonetic imitation that slows reading without adding identity or rhythm; or narration identifying a source Ira cannot yet know.

Test: identify the baseline, foreground and background sources, source location/distance, masking/occlusion, what Ira hears versus infers, what shifts attention, and what changes because of the sound. Remove details that do no perceptual, narrative, spatial, social, or rhythmic work.

## 6. World depth / game-ness

Current hypothesis: a world feels large not merely because lore claims it is large, but because the visible moment implies independent systems beyond the protagonist.

**Lore quantity ≠ experienced world scale.**

Potential signals: infrastructure and transport; people with independent purposes; institutions with procedures and friction; districts with distinct functions; factions and social boundaries; supply, maintenance, commerce, and services; local history embedded in material conditions; power systems with consistent constraints and consequences; cultures and populations not existing solely to explain themselves to Ira; traces of yesterday's events; and activity continuing outside his view.

‘Game-ness’ means **explorable world logic**, not HUDs, quests, status menus, or literal game UI. Readers should feel that if Ira went elsewhere, the world would continue to contain systems, people, and consequences.

This is a project design hypothesis requiring more research and testing, not a finished worldbuilding specification. Do not use it to invent canon or dump all world information into early chapters.
