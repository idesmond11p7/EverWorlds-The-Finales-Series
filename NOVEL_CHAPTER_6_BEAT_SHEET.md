# EverWorlds Chapter 6 — Beat Sheet & Dependency Map

## Target
**Working length:** 3,000–4,000 words.

This is a pacing target, not a hard word-count requirement. The chapter earns length through lived movement rather than exposition.

## Starting State

- Ira has reached Kuoh Academy in Chapter 5.
- The entrance examination is tomorrow.
- He has no supernatural framework.
- He has no Ira memories.
- He has only a basic practical understanding of his new situation.
- He has somewhere to return to: the apartment.

## Beat 1 — Wake / Apartment Anchor

**Purpose:** Convert the apartment from “someone else's room” into a physical place Ira temporarily inhabits.

Show:
- morning light;
- objects he has already handled;
- clothing/preparation;
- food/drink;
- small practical decisions;
- the body's unfamiliarity becoming less shocking.

Do not repeat Chapter 5's mirror discovery.

**Character movement:** from displaced → functional.

## Beat 2 — Preparation Problem

Ira realizes he needs to leave early enough to avoid another navigation disaster.

He checks:
- exam documentation;
- money;
- route;
- school materials;
- time.

The period setting should appear naturally through objects rather than exposition.

**Character movement:** functional → cautiously competent.

## Beat 3 — Immediate Neighbourhood

He leaves the apartment.

The neighbourhood is rendered through:
- building materials;
- utility infrastructure;
- shops;
- pedestrians;
- traffic;
- weather;
- food smells;
- sounds at different distances.

No “Kuoh is beautiful” declaration.

The reader learns the place through Ira's movement.

## Beat 4 — Local Station

Ira reaches the station.

Important:
- ticket purchase;
- platform;
- timetable;
- crowd;
- announcement;
- waiting;
- train arrival.

The station establishes that this journey has physical logistics.

## Beat 5 — First Long Transit Segment

The city changes gradually.

Use the train window and carriage interior to create spatial progression.

Do not catalogue landmarks.

Ira notices:
- density changes;
- building height;
- vegetation;
- road layout;
- industrial/commercial/residential shifts;
- people getting on/off.

The world should feel larger than the protagonist.

## Beat 6 — Food / Ordinary Interaction

A mundane stop creates an opportunity for Ira to interact with someone.

Possible function:
- buying food;
- asking directions;
- dealing with a vending machine/shop;
- brief misunderstanding.

The interaction should not secretly be supernatural.

**Purpose:** establish ordinary social friction and Ira's new identity in public.

## Beat 7 — Transfer

Ira changes trains or routes.

The transfer teaches him:
- where he is;
- how Japanese transit works;
- what the city feels like outside the first neighbourhood.

Avoid explaining transit systems to the reader.

Show the procedure.

## Beat 8 — Getting Lost

Ira makes a believable navigation mistake.

Important:
- not identical to Chapter 5's repeated “I am lost” gag;
- this mistake should be more subtle;
- he recovers using something he has learned.

This demonstrates character growth.

**Character movement:** cautious competence → self-reliance.

## Beat 9 — Kuoh Approach

The physical environment begins changing.

Do not announce the reason.

Use:
- lower density;
- different architecture;
- greenery;
- wider streets;
- school-age population;
- shops;
- residential spaces;
- subtle local identity.

The reader should begin forming a stable spatial model of Kuoh.

## Beat 10 — Academy Perimeter

Ira approaches the Academy.

The school should feel like a real institution:
- gates;
- staff;
- students;
- parents/visitors;
- administrative activity;
- signs;
- buildings;
- movement.

Avoid anime-school spectacle.

## Beat 11 — Registration / Arrival

Ira handles the administrative process.

This is deliberately mundane.

Show:
- where to go;
- what he is asked for;
- documents;
- waiting;
- other applicants;
- small social observations.

This makes the later supernatural world feel more intrusive when it arrives.

## Beat 12 — Sona Glimpse

Sona appears briefly.

Ira does not know:
- her identity;
- her authority;
- her supernatural nature;
- her importance.

He simply notices a student/young woman whose presence registers for some concrete reason.

No lore.

No “I could tell she was powerful.”

No authorial foreshadowing disguised as POV.

The scene should be explainable as an ordinary encounter at this stage.

## Beat 13 — Final Pre-Exam Space

Ira finds where he needs to wait.

He has a few minutes to observe the school.

This is where the chapter can briefly widen:
- students;
- architecture;
- campus vegetation;
- ambient noise;
- weather/light;
- distant conversations.

The reader should now possess a stronger mental map than at the beginning.

## Beat 14 — 9:00 AM

The bell rings.

The social environment changes.

Applicants move.

Staff begin the examination.

Ira realizes:

**This is it.**

### CUT.

Do not start the examination.

## Information Budget

### Reader gains
- practical feel of Ira's apartment/neighbourhood;
- physical experience of the route to Kuoh;
- basic sense of Kuoh;
- Academy as a functioning institution;
- Sona exists and is physically present;
- the world contains unusually striking people/visual details without explanation.

### Ira gains
- practical navigation knowledge;
- confidence functioning in the new body;
- first ordinary social interactions in public;
- familiarity with Kuoh's physical environment;
- knowledge of the Academy as a place he may attend.

### Reader does NOT gain
- supernatural exposition;
- faction hierarchy;
- Rias/Sona authority;
- Sacred Gear information;
- Work Make terminology;
- power-system explanation;
- hidden-world mechanics.

## Dependency Map

**Apartment**
→ establishes starting location

**Route**
→ establishes physical geography

**Transit**
→ establishes scale

**Public interaction**
→ establishes social reality

**Kuoh**
→ establishes local environment

**Academy**
→ establishes immediate social institution

**Sona glimpse**
→ introduces first named-world proximity without explanation

**Bell**
→ hands control to Chapter 7

## Rendering Audit

Each major location must contain enough information for the reader to answer:

- Where is Ira?
- What is immediately around him?
- What is farther away?
- What is moving?
- What is the dominant light source?
- What surfaces react to the light?
- What can he hear?
- Where are those sounds coming from?
- What does he actually notice?

Do not answer all eight mechanically in every paragraph.

The purpose is to maintain a coherent internal world model.

## Anti-Repetition Check

Do not repeat:
- Chapter 5's mirror discovery;
- Chapter 5's basic “lost in Kuoh” joke;
- Chapter 5's first astonishment at the city;
- Chapter 5's emotional grief sequence;
- Chapter 5's identity discovery.

Chapter 6 assumes those have happened.

## Chapter-Level Emotional Curve

**uncertainty**
→ **routine**
→ **curiosity**
→ **minor frustration**
→ **competence**
→ **environmental absorption**
→ **anticipation**
→ **exam threshold**

The chapter should feel increasingly alive rather than increasingly ominous.

## Handoff

Chapter 7 starts immediately from the examination beginning.

Chapter 8 begins only after the examination has produced the necessary conditions for the first power plotline.
