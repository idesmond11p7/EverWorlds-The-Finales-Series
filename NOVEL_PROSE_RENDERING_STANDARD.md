# EverWorlds Prose Rendering & Perceptual World Standard

## Status
**Active craft standard**

## Core Principle
EverWorlds prose should make the reader **render the world**, not merely tell the reader what the world looks like.

Narrative comprehension research supports the idea that readers construct and update situation models containing spatial, temporal, causal, character, and object information. Spatial information is tracked relative to the protagonist's location and perspective, and readers update those models as events and locations change. citeturn0search0turn0search1turn0search10

This is the literary equivalent of a rendering pipeline.

## The Rendering Stack

Every environment is internally evaluated through:

1. **Space** — where the observer is; where objects are; what blocks what.
2. **Light** — actual source, direction, softness, intensity, reflection.
3. **Material** — concrete, glass, metal, fabric, wood, water, plastic, skin, etc.
4. **Atmosphere** — humidity, rain, dust, haze, mist, heat, clear air.
5. **Motion** — protagonist, pedestrians, vehicles, weather, objects continuing independently.
6. **Sound** — source, distance, obstruction, direction, persistence.
7. **Colour** — specific and contextual; not automatically bright or saturated.
8. **Attention** — what the viewpoint character actually notices.
9. **Interpretation** — what the character thinks it means.
10. **Cadence** — sentence/paragraph rhythm controlling how quickly attention travels through the rendered space.

## Lighting Rule

Never let an adjective decide the lighting when physical conditions can do it.

Avoid relying on:
- beautiful street;
- cheerful morning;
- dark city;
- magical atmosphere;
- gloomy room.

Instead establish the physical inputs that produce the perception.

**Bad:** The street was beautiful.

**Better:** Rainwater remained along the curb. Shop signs broke across the surface whenever cars passed.

The second does not require darkness. It supplies reflective surfaces, artificial light, movement, and spatial relationships, allowing the reader's situation model to construct the visual scene.

## Brightness Is Not Beauty

**Beautiful ≠ bright.**

EverWorlds may use:
- muted greens;
- deep blues;
- warm amber;
- pale gold;
- wet charcoal;
- faded reds;
- soft skin tones;
- silver-grey skies;
- rich natural vegetation.

Visual richness is not neon saturation.

A bright scene can feel lonely.
A dark scene can feel safe.
A rainy scene can feel peaceful.
A sunny scene can feel threatening.

**Lighting and emotional valence are independent variables.**

## Cadence as Perceptual Control

Sentence rhythm changes how the reader traverses the situation model.

### Environmental scan
Use flowing sentences and substantial paragraphs to move attention across space.

### Normal movement
Use varied sentence lengths and connective transitions.

### Narrowed attention
Shorter sentences may concentrate attention when the viewpoint character focuses on one stimulus.

### Shock / sudden perception
A short paragraph or isolated sentence can create an event boundary.

### Horror
Whitespace and shorter units may interrupt an established rhythm, but only when the character's perception genuinely contracts or becomes unstable.

### Do Not
Turn ordinary prose into constant:
> I looked.  
> I paused.  
> Nothing.  
> I stared.

That is artificial suspense, not realism.

## Typography

Typography is normally invisible.

Use:
- ordinary paragraph flow;
- natural dialogue grouping;
- occasional isolated lines;
- deliberate paragraph breaks.

Use unusual formatting only when it has a perceptual/narrative function.

White space can function as an attentional boundary. Research on event segmentation indicates that changes in character, space, and other situational dimensions influence when readers update their situation models. citeturn0search10

Therefore typography is treated as a **special effect**, not decoration.

## POV Rendering

First-person prose must render from the character's perceptual position.

The narrator may:
- observe;
- hear;
- smell;
- infer;
- remember;
- hypothesize;
- misunderstand.

The narrator must not render information that the character has no route to perceive or know.

**Observation ≠ explanation.**

## Environmental Continuity

The world continues independently of the protagonist.

People:
- walk somewhere;
- work;
- argue;
- wait;
- eat;
- make mistakes;
- ignore Ira;
- pursue their own concerns.

Objects:
- remain where they were placed;
- become wet/dry;
- cast reflections/shadows;
- obstruct movement;
- accumulate wear.

Weather:
- changes surfaces;
- changes sound;
- changes visibility;
- changes people's behaviour.

This is the prose equivalent of the lived-world philosophy behind grounded open-world games. Red Dead Redemption's development explicitly used NPC schedules, distinct behaviours, researched environments, and ecological rules to avoid a world feeling like a collection of static decorations. citeturn0search45

## EverWorlds Visual Target

The target is **grounded perceptual fidelity**, not generic cinematic spectacle.

Primary references:
- Red Dead Redemption / Red Dead Redemption 2
- Kingdom Come: Deliverance / Deliverance II
- grounded realistic horror-game environments
- Eternum's environmental immediacy where useful

The desired feeling is:
**“I could physically exist here.”**

Not:
**“The author wants me to think this location is cool.”**

## RTX Definition

RTX is a shorthand for **increased perceptual resolution**, not brightness.

It means:
- clearer material distinctions;
- stronger spatial depth;
- more independent environmental activity;
- richer natural lighting;
- more precise colour;
- better atmospheric layering;
- more physical consequences;
- more coherent spaces.

The reader should experience the increase rather than be told that the world has “RTX.”

## Escalation

The world should not begin at maximum visual intensity.

Progression:

**Grounded ordinary reality**
→ **richer perceptual detail**
→ **subtle unusual beauty**
→ **increasingly stylized physical reality**
→ **supernatural visual grammar**
→ **full supernatural spectacle**

If everything is extraordinary from page one, nothing produces contrast.

## Chapter 6 Application

Chapter 6 is the first major test of this standard.

Its job is not to announce the DxD world.

Its job is to make Ira's new world **inhabitable**.

The reader should learn the world through:
- apartment details;
- preparation;
- streets;
- transit;
- stations;
- food;
- weather;
- buildings;
- people;
- school surroundings;
- mistakes;
- waiting;
- distance;
- ordinary inconvenience.

The journey must feel traversed rather than summarized.

The unusual qualities of the world should accumulate incidentally.

No one needs to tell Ira that the world is visually heightened.

## Pre-Draft Rendering Audit

Before writing an environment, answer internally:

- Where is Ira?
- What is nearest?
- What is farthest?
- What blocks his view?
- What is the primary light source?
- What surfaces respond to it?
- What is moving?
- What can he hear?
- Where does the sound originate?
- What is the air like?
- What colours are actually present?
- What does Ira notice first?
- What does he ignore?
- What physical action changes the scene?

Then write only what belongs in the narrative.

## Final Rule

**Render conditions. Let perception emerge.**

Do not substitute aesthetic labels for physical reality.
