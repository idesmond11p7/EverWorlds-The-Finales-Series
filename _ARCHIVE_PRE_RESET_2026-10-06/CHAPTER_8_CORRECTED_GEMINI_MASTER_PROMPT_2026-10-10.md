## 6. The prose standard: make the reader experience the scene

The project's central prose doctrine is: **do not merely report what happened; make the reader experience what happened.**

This is not a request for more adjectives. It requires continuous causal and spatial rendering.

### Physical continuity
The reader should be able to track Ira's position, movement, nearby people and objects, what changes, what resists him, and why the next action follows. Objects should usually enter the prose through interaction or consequence: weight, friction, temperature, texture, obstruction, use, sound, motion, or effect on the body. Do not present a room or street as an inventory and then explain what it means.

### Persistent scene
Do not reset the mental camera after every sentence. A place continues to exist while Ira's attention shifts. Let sounds be blocked, reflected, muffled, or carried by the environment where the actual setting supports it. Let movement reveal space gradually.

### Attention and cognition
The world is not smaller because Ira notices less. Ira notices less because his attention is finite. Select details according to his current goal, memory, anxiety, curiosity, or mistake. His intelligence supports transfer reasoning but does not make every inference correct. An incorrect inference should have a believable cause and consequence.

### Causality and friction
A scene should move through action, environmental response, social response, redirection, and consequence—not a chain of observations followed by explanations. Use ordinary friction only when the situation naturally creates it. Do not manufacture obstacles to satisfy a beat sheet, and do not remove every inconvenience to rush to a clue.

### Paragraph architecture
Paragraphs are perceptual/discourse units, not arbitrary readability breaks. Group causally related perceptions, actions, dialogue, and consequences into developed paragraphs. Single-sentence paragraphs are reserved for genuinely exceptional emphasis, not used as a default rhythm. Do not create a wall of text either. Preserve novel typography and natural dialogue spacing.

### Cadence and metaphor
Use medium/elastic pulp prose: clear physical vectors, visceral sensory anchors, dramatic momentum, and enough room for atmosphere when the scene earns it. During quiet reflection, allow thought to emerge from the present environment. During movement, let the physical scene keep moving. Under pressure, tighten cause and effect. Metaphor is an accent, not the operating system. Avoid repeated “like/as if,” abstract summaries, faux-cinematic fragments, generic AI aphorisms, and constant filter constructions (“I looked,” “I saw,” “I noticed”) when direct perception or action can carry the moment.

### First-person epistemic limits
Ira may describe what he senses, remembers, believes, infers, or suspects. Keep those categories distinct. Do not let the narrator state as fact what he cannot know. Do not smuggle authorial explanations into his perception.
