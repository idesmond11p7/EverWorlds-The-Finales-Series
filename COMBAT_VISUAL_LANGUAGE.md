# Combat Visual Language — EverWorlds: The Finales

## Status

**Confirmed working canon / visual-design direction**

This document records a visual and experiential principle being carried from the EverWorlds narrative into the eventual game/system presentation. It is not a universal attack template and must not flatten different characters or power systems into one visual language.

## Core Principle

EverWorlds combat should not communicate power only through magnitude.

Different supernatural expressions should have different **visual grammars** based on what the phenomenon actually is, how it behaves, and what it means to the character.

A powerful attack can therefore be:

- loud or quiet
- explosive or surgical
- physically forceful or visually transformative
- fast or deliberately cinematic
- environmentally destructive or environmentally subtle

The presentation should communicate the **identity of the underlying phenomenon**, not merely make every ultimate attack bigger.

## Rias / Power of Destruction — Visual Grammar

Rias's Power of Destruction is an example of a distinct combat language.

It should not be presented as a generic colored beam/Kamehameha analogue.

It should also not be reduced to a sterile universal "delete button."

Its visual identity combines:

**stealth + precision + demonic destruction + accelerating ravaging + beautiful mythic aftermath.**

### Deployment

The Power of Destruction has a very short activation/deployment window.

Once a target is visually acquired or otherwise validly locked/acquired by the relevant targeting conditions, the destructive manifestation can appear at its intended size and form without a prolonged charge sequence.

The manifestation is not required to be beam-shaped.

Possible manifestations may use compact, natural forms such as spheres/orbs or other appropriate shapes. Shape should follow the Workcraft's established expression rather than being forced into a laser aesthetic.

The attack's danger comes partly from its low telegraphing:

- very quiet relative to attacks such as Akeno's large-scale lightning expressions
- little or no conventional wind-up
- very fast reaction/deployment time
- long effective engagement range
- minimal obvious trajectory/telegraphing
- target acquisition can support supernatural aim/locking behavior

This is a combat identity, not literal videogame aim-assist terminology inside the fiction.

## Environmental Expression

The environment can react strongly **in color and appearance without being physically battered**.

The Power of Destruction may cause surrounding colors to shift or become saturated:

- crimson
- violet
- magenta
- pink
- white-hot highlights
- darkened or color-contaminated shadows
- colored reflections and atmospheric tint

The environment does not need to shake, explode, or experience a large kinetic shockwave.

**Visual disturbance does not automatically mean physical disturbance.**

## Destruction Progression

A successful hit can behave like an accelerating destructive condition.

The initial effect may be restrained and elegant.

As the destructive process continues, it becomes increasingly ravenous and difficult for the target to resist.

The progression is:

**contact → onset → spreading destruction → acceleration → ravaging → catastrophic finish**

The target should look increasingly helpless as the destruction overtakes it.

This is distinct from Akeno's overwhelming-force presentation:

- **Akeno:** the attack overwhelms the target through force and spectacle.
- **Rias:** the destructive phenomenon takes hold of the target and progressively ravages it.

The final violent/explosive stage is therefore the result of the target being overwhelmed by destruction, not evidence that the Power of Destruction is simply an explosive projectile.

## Demonic Expression

The Power of Destruction should retain a distinctly demonic character.

Its destructive energy may contain fleeting face-like or soul-like forms, screaming mouths, distorted silhouettes, or other manifestations that suggest something ravenous exists within the phenomenon.

These manifestations should not turn the attack into conventional horror imagery.

Their defining quality is that they can be **visually loud while remaining acoustically restrained**.

The result should feel as though something is screaming inside the destruction without the battlefield necessarily hearing a conventional scream.

## Mythic Kill / After-Kill

The elimination does not end when the target disappears.

A separate after-kill visual language can follow the destruction.

The signature after-effect may form from **petal-like pieces of destructive energy**, colored in the same saturated crimson/violet/magenta/white family.

The petals can:

- bloom around the location of the defeated target
- carry delicate crackling energy
- orbit or fold around a central point
- briefly form a recognizable signature/marker
- preserve a visual trace of the target's former presence

The signature is not merely decoration. Its eventual mechanical meaning is intentionally left to later system design.

For the visual layer, it should read as something beautiful left behind by an otherwise ravenous act of destruction.

The signature then gradually fades:

**petals separate → crackling weakens → colors dim → signature thins → final fragment disappears**

The after-kill should feel like a deliberate Mythic-style flourish rather than ordinary lingering particles.

## Cross-Character Contrast

Akeno and Rias should not share the same ultimate-attack grammar.

### Akeno

- expansive
- storm-driven
- luminous
- loud
- environmentally expressive
- overwhelming
- long cinematic build
- large finishing discharge

### Rias

- stealth-oriented
- compact manifestation
- extremely fast deployment
- colorful without requiring kinetic environmental destruction
- demonic
- initially controlled
- increasingly ravenous
- destructive/ravaging finish
- distinctive mythic after-kill signature

The contrast is intentional.

EverWorlds should preserve these differences rather than standardizing all supernatural attacks around one spectacle formula.

## Game / Narrative Relationship

The same underlying reality should support both the novel's prose representation and the eventual game presentation.

The game may abbreviate or accelerate individual combat events for playability, while the narrative can expand the same phenomenon into detailed prose.

The underlying visual grammar should remain consistent.

The goal is not to make the novel imitate a videogame literally.

The goal is for the game and narrative to express the **same underlying supernatural reality through different presentation layers**.
