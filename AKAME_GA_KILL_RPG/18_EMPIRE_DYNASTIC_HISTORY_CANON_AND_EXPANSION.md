# 18. Imperial Dynastic History: Canon Anchors and Original Bridges
Status: canon/secondary-report/original expansion separated. This document fills historical gaps for RPG use without claiming invented emperors are manga canon.

## Evidence key
- **MANGA-ANCHORED:** supported by the manga's broad premise or depicted events.
- **SECONDARY REPORT:** summarized in fan reference material and still needing direct panel/page verification.
- **ORIGINAL BRIDGE:** invented connective history for this RPG, designed not to contradict established events.
- **OPEN:** chronology, identity, date or interpretation unresolved.

## Firm historical anchors
1. **First Emperor — MANGA-ANCHORED / SECONDARY REPORT.** The founder establishes the Empire and commissions the original 48 Teigu to protect its future. The project gathers exceptional artisans, engineers, alchemists, mystics and rare materials, including Danger Beast-derived components and Orichalcum. The Wakoku smiths are associated with Murasame's creation; the details of every workshop and contributor need source checking. citeturn0search0turn0search2
2. **Imperial expansion — INTERPRETATION.** Teigu provide an enormous strategic advantage and are associated with Imperial expansion. Do not reduce every conquest to Teigu alone; logistics, administration, terrain, alliances and other technologies matter.
3. **The Great Civil War — SECONDARY REPORT.** Several centuries before the main story, a major civil war causes nearly half of the original Teigu to be lost. Its causes, factions, dates and full political consequences are insufficiently detailed in the references consulted. Do not invent a “canon” cause. citeturn0search0turn0search2
4. **The Shingu Emperor, about 400 years before the main story — SECONDARY REPORT.** An emperor attempts to create superior weapons using Imperial resources. The resulting Shingu are judged inferior to the original Teigu and sealed; later they are issued to the Empire's Elite Seven assassination unit. The exact emperor's personal name and the detailed chronology remain OPEN. citeturn0search4turn0search2
5. **Current-era young Emperor — MANGA-ANCHORED.** The young Emperor is the surviving heir and can operate Shikoutazer. Prime Minister Honest manipulates him and secretly kills his parents, leaving the boy politically isolated and inexperienced. This is not evidence that the child is inherently evil. citeturn0search22
6. **Honest's capture of the state — MANGA-ANCHORED.** Honest uses court influence, patronage, coercion and murder to control government. The Empire's institutions predate him; he exploits and corrupts them rather than creating every institution from nothing.
7. **Manga ending — MANGA-ANCHORED.** The manga's end diverges substantially from the anime. Campaign continuity must follow the manga. The RPG must not use anime-only deaths or outcomes as manga facts. citeturn0search3turn0search23

## Dynastic chronology framework
Exact regnal years and personal names are not securely established by the secondary sources used here. Therefore this is a structured sequence of eras, not a fabricated list of “official emperors.” The project may add named rulers as **ORIGINAL BRIDGE** entries once the chronology is checked against the manga and the desired campaign date.

### Era A — Founding Consolidation
**Anchor:** First Emperor, original Teigu project.
**Original bridge:** The founding regime builds roads, granaries, military depots, census offices, regional courts and a system for acquiring rare materials. These institutions are practical necessities for governing a large realm. The First Emperor's long-term legacy is mixed: common standards and infrastructure enable trade, while central extraction gives later rulers tools for domination.
**Unresolved:** exact founding date, personal name, birthplace, territorial extent at founding, succession method, lifespan and whether the Empire's later borders resemble its original borders.

### Era B — Expansion and Administrative Standardization
**Anchor:** Empire becomes a major power; Teigu are an important military advantage.
**Original bridge:** Successive rulers establish regional governorates, frontier commands and roads that link resource districts to military depots. Conquered regions retain some local law when cheap to govern and face direct administration where taxation, mines or strategic passes demand tighter control. This produces a patchwork empire, not a perfectly uniform state.
**Causal consequences:** long roads lower military response times but also allow epidemics and rebellion to travel; standard weights support trade and taxation; garrisons create markets but consume local food; frontier settlements develop hybrid cultures.
**Unresolved:** which named regions were conquered under which ruler.

### Era C — The Great Civil War
**Anchor:** A civil war centuries before the story results in the loss of nearly half the Teigu, according to secondary references.
**Original bridge, explicitly non-canon:** the RPG may use a multi-sided succession conflict involving rival court blocs, regional commands and local autonomists, but must not assert that this was the manga's confirmed cause. Competing historical schools may disagree. Some records are destroyed; later rulers exploit the uncertainty to justify centralization.
**Persistent world effects:** lost relics create treasure markets and false claims; old fortifications remain in border landscapes; veterans' settlements and abandoned supply roads persist; regional elites retain suspicion of the capital.
**Do not:** decide all missing Teigu were destroyed; the secondary report says lost, not necessarily destroyed.

### Era D — Restoration and Controlled Recovery
**Original bridge:** postwar rulers rebuild tax routes and redefine the Imperial succession. A “recovery” narrative gives the court legitimacy, but reconstruction burdens differ by province. Some towns profit from depots and roads; others lose autonomy to emergency administrators.
**Institutions that may emerge:** a relic registry, provincial audit offices, military engineering corps, archive custodians and restricted alchemical workshops. These are original additions and should be assigned only where useful.
**Failure mode:** emergency powers become permanent; inventories of relics drift from reality; local elites falsify reports to avoid confiscation.

### Era E — The Shingu Attempt, approximately 400 years before the story
**Anchor:** an emperor seeks weapons to surpass the Teigu; the resulting Shingu are inferior and sealed, later being issued to the Elite Seven.
**Original bridge:** the failed program creates competing interpretations: some engineers believe the project failed because its materials were inferior; some mystics believe its design philosophy misunderstood the original Teigu; some officials believe the court expected a political miracle from technical work. These are later theories, not confirmed explanations.
**Long-term effects:** restricted archives, specialist lineages, rumors of sealed weapons, and institutional fear of unauthorized relic research.

### Era F — Long Imperial Routine
**Original bridge:** several generations of rulers inherit the machinery of state without possessing the founding emperor's prestige. The Empire survives through taxes, law, roads, local bargains, military coercion and accumulated institutional habit. Some emperors reform administration; others spend political capital on court factions or border campaigns.
**Key rule:** not every emperor is a genius, tyrant or reformer. Most rulers have limited information, competing advisers and inherited constraints.
**Suggested generation templates for future named rulers:** consolidator; border administrator; religious patron; debt-financed builder; cautious diplomat; failed reformer; military regent; archive obsessive; short-lived child ruler; provincial compromise-maker. These are archetypes for original additions, not canon characters.

### Era G — The Present Dynasty's Final Generation
**Anchor:** the young Emperor inherits the throne after Honest secretly kills his parents. Honest controls much of the court and steers state violence for his own benefit.
**Original bridge:** the regime's apparent strength hides brittle dependencies: Honest's patronage web, corrupt revenue extraction, factions that fear one another, provincial commanders with local interests, and a young sovereign lacking independent information. The state does not collapse because every official is evil; it collapses because incentives, violence and bad information compound.
**Manga boundary:** preserve the manga's actual sequence and ending. Do not insert original emperors, wars or external invasions into the canon incident unless the player's actions create a plausible interaction.

## How to author named emperors between the First Emperor and the current Emperor
Create an entry only when it serves a real historical dependency. Required fields:
- Name and reign window (clearly marked original unless manga-supported).
- Succession mechanism and legitimacy claim.
- Known world conditions and information available to the ruler.
- Two or more institutions inherited.
- One reform or policy with material costs.
- One group that benefits and one that loses.
- A consequence persisting into later eras.
- Relationship to Teigu/Shingu policy, without inventing new Teigu.
- Confidence label and evidence/fiction tag.
- Explicit note if the entry is a gap-filling original ruler.

Avoid a fake precise 1,000-year list with a name for every year. The series' broad thousand-year imperial span does not provide a verified regnal calendar. A few well-constructed, clearly labeled historical rulers and disputed traditions are more internally coherent than false precision.

## Historical memory and competing accounts
Different institutions preserve different versions:
- Capital court annals emphasize continuity and the First Emperor's foresight.
- Provincial records emphasize tax burdens, local autonomy and conscription.
- Frontier songs preserve raids and abandoned forts but distort dates.
- Teigu custodians focus on artifact provenance and losses.
- Religious orders reinterpret civil wars as moral parables.
- Merchant archives record tolls, weights and route closures more reliably than court motives.
- Underground communities may preserve older settlement memories but have their own gaps and biases.
No source is automatically omniscient. Give each account a provenance, motive, transmission path and confidence.

## Canon safeguards
- Do not name the First Emperor unless the manga provides a name.
- Do not present original intermediate emperors as official canon.
- Do not assign the Great Civil War a definitive cause without source evidence.
- Keep the approximately 400-year Shingu attempt distinct from the older civil war unless the source establishes a link.
- Do not claim the current Emperor created the Empire's institutions.
- Follow manga, not anime, continuity.
