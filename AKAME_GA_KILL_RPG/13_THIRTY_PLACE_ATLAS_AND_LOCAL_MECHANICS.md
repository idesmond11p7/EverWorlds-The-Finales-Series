# 13 — Thirty-Place Atlas: Distinct Local Worlds, Not Reskinned Backdrops

**Status:** Original expansion. These are authored place seeds, not manga geography. Their coordinates and exact relationships remain provisional until the planetary atlas is mapped. The world-scale brief sets the planet at approximately **12.3 times Earth's surface area** as an authorial scale target; that figure is not a claim about *Akame ga Kill!* canon. The setting's gravity is a world rule to define later, not a reason to derail this atlas into real-world physics debate.

## Place identity contract

A location is not distinct merely because its color palette changes. Every major place needs a different set of physical affordances, movement constraints, sightlines, sound behavior, resource flows, hazards, social rules and strategic pressures. Its layout should shape what people do there.

Every entry below is a design seed. Before it becomes a final map, record its neighbors, elevation, water sources, prevailing weather, routes, population, ownership, construction history, utility networks, daily rhythms, information routes, and persistent state.

## A. Rivers, wetlands and flood-built civilization

### PLC-001 — Ors Vey, the Seven-Rain Seat
**Terrain:** A broad river confluence on raised limestone terraces.  
**Layout:** Seven radial embankments divide the city into flood-height zones; bridges connect administrative islands, while poorer housing occupies lower terraces.  
**Visual identity:** Pale stone, copper roofs, black flood marks and long blue awnings.  
**Movement:** Boats can bypass some street congestion, but current and bridge closures matter. During floods, the lowest routes disappear first.  
**Sound/light:** Water reflects light into covered lanes; bells carry across the river but echo under stone bridges.  
**Economy/politics:** Archive work, barges, grain contracts and flood engineering. The city survives through negotiated water management.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A breached levee displaces residents, reroutes boats, contaminates wells and moves the political center toward higher ground.

### PLC-002 — The Reed Crown
**Terrain:** A delta settlement on artificial mounds between tidal channels.  
**Layout:** Granaries and court buildings stand on high platforms; working docks shift with channel depth. Districts are connected by raised causeways and small ferries.  
**Visual identity:** Reed gold, brine silver, lacquer red, pale mudbrick and tall sluice poles.  
**Movement:** A route that is walkable at low tide may be a boat route later. Tide schedules affect markets and patrols.  
**Sound/light:** Reeds dampen distant sound; open water exposes movement against the horizon.  
**Economy/politics:** Grain, salt, fish and boat tolls. Water gates are political infrastructure.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A damaged sluice changes salinity, land value, crop yield and evacuation routes over following seasons.

### PLC-003 — Namaru's Split Market
**Terrain:** A floodplain market where two branches of a river reunite.  
**Layout:** Separate dry-season and flood-season market grids overlap; some stalls are designed to be dismantled and carried to upper terraces.  
**Visual identity:** Red clay, wet jade, pale timber, deep green awnings and painted water-level poles.  
**Movement:** Goods move by shallow boats during high water and carts during low water. Mud and current change travel time.  
**Sound/light:** Rain on thousands of roof panels creates a near-continuous roar that masks quiet speech.  
**Economy/politics:** Fish, grain, medicine, river transport and repair services.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A new upstream diversion changes sediment deposits and eventually moves the best dock frontage.

### PLC-004 — Bellwater Steps
**Terrain:** A steep town descending toward a cold, fast river.  
**Layout:** Terraced stair streets connect workshops above to mills and ferry landings below. Water channels run beside selected stairways.  
**Visual identity:** Slate-blue stone, white limewash, iron railings and moss-dark mill walls.  
**Movement:** Vertical movement is the central cost. Heavy carts use switchback ramps; stairs are faster for pedestrians but dangerous when wet.  
**Sound/light:** Mill noise masks voices near the river; spray creates bright halos in morning light.  
**Economy/politics:** Milling, paper, metal finishing and water-powered machinery.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A mill failure can flood a lower lane, halt grain processing and create a food shortage upstream.

### PLC-005 — The Silt Cathedral
**Terrain:** A half-buried ancient settlement at a river's abandoned bend.  
**Layout:** New houses perch on old roofs and raised causeways; lower halls are entered only during dry periods or by boat.  
**Visual identity:** Silt-grey brick, oxidized green metal, ochre cloth and carved doorways half below ground.  
**Movement:** Familiar routes can be blocked by seasonal sediment or collapse. Lower levels are hazardous and poorly mapped.  
**Sound/light:** Subterranean water carries sound unpredictably; sunlight enters through shafts and broken roofs.  
**Economy/politics:** Salvage, pottery, river trade and contested antiquities.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** Excavation can drain a cistern, undermine a street or expose a record that changes property claims.

## B. Mountains, mines and high passes

### PLC-006 — Kharad's Nine Hearths
**Terrain:** A chain of linked mining towns built around separate furnaces and ore seams.  
**Layout:** Each hearth has a distinct working quarter, slag yard, water channel and workers' housing; ridge roads connect them.  
**Visual identity:** Basalt black, furnace orange, iron-grey roofs and pale survey marks.  
**Movement:** Narrow switchbacks limit carts; smoke and heat make some routes unpleasant or unsafe.  
**Sound/light:** Hammering echoes between cliff faces; furnace glow dominates night scenes.  
**Economy/politics:** Iron, tools, structural fittings and foundry labor.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A furnace shutdown disrupts repairs and weapons supply; slag heaps can alter runoff and contaminate lower streams.

### PLC-007 — Rimegate Pass
**Terrain:** A narrow saddle between avalanche-prone slopes.  
**Layout:** A gatehouse, shelter halls, signal towers, snow walls and a switchback road occupy the safest rock.  
**Visual identity:** Cold granite, pine-black timber, white limewash and rust-red roof tiles.  
**Movement:** Wind exposure, snow depth, ice and pack-animal limits decide who can pass. A closed route may force a multi-day detour.  
**Sound/light:** Snow absorbs some sounds; bells and avalanche cracks travel strangely across the bowl.  
**Economy/politics:** Tolls, rescue services, shelter stores and mountain trade.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A collapse can isolate villages, delay messages and redirect military or merchant traffic for weeks.

### PLC-008 — The Hanging Quarry
**Terrain:** A stone extraction town carved into a steep escarpment.  
**Layout:** Crane platforms, anchored walkways, spoil chutes and switchback staircases connect quarry faces to workshops below.  
**Visual identity:** Dust-white rock, rusted chains, saffron safety cloth and deep shadowed cuts.  
**Movement:** Height, falling debris and narrow crossings constrain movement. Heavy loads travel by crane, not by hand-carried shortcut.  
**Sound/light:** Echoes obscure the location of a hammer strike; dust shafts make some sightlines visible and others blinding.  
**Economy/politics:** Building stone, lime, sculpture and quarry engineering.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A damaged anchor closes a route, traps workers and sends debris into a lower neighborhood.

### PLC-009 — Underbell
**Terrain:** A subterranean city in connected caverns, not a single giant empty cave.  
**Layout:** Habitable chambers connect through marked tunnels, ventilation shafts, water cisterns and pressure doors. Districts are separated by geology and airflow.  
**Visual identity:** Dark stone, bronze bellwork, mineral whites and controlled pools of amber light.  
**Movement:** Tunnel width, air quality, water levels and gate schedules determine routes. A short map distance may require a long vertical detour.  
**Sound/light:** Sound travels far along some tunnels and dies at bends; light sources are strategic resources.  
**Economy/politics:** Mining, fungi cultivation, mineral processing, underground transit and specialized craft.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A collapsed passage changes air circulation, contaminates water or isolates a district.

### PLC-010 — The White Switchbacks
**Terrain:** A high-altitude pilgrimage town built on pale scree and limestone shelves.  
**Layout:** Winding paths pass shrines, weather shelters and small terraced farms; no single broad street crosses the town.  
**Visual identity:** Chalk-white stone, indigo cloth, wind-polished bells and sparse red doors.  
**Movement:** Thin air, steep grades, loose stone and weather affect exertion and footing. Pack animals use separate routes.  
**Sound/light:** Wind erases quiet speech; hard sun creates sharp shadows and strong glare.  
**Economy/politics:** Pilgrimage, medicinal herbs, guide services and mountain surveys.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A landslide blocks a shrine route, shifts pilgrimage income and forces competing communities to share a shelter.

## C. Forest, marsh and ecological frontiers

### PLC-011 — The Keth Rootwalks
**Terrain:** A settlement network on elevated ground among giant old-growth trees and wet forest.  
**Layout:** Platforms and bridges connect selected trees, while ground-level routes remain essential for water, farming and animal movement. Not every tree is a building.  
**Visual identity:** Wet bark, jade canopy, red earth, woven shade screens and rain-dark timber.  
**Movement:** Height, rope bridges, slippery roots and canopy gaps matter. A bridge has a load limit and can fail.  
**Sound/light:** Canopy layers scatter light; rain and insects make distance hard to judge.  
**Economy/politics:** Forest stewardship, medicines, resin, weaving and carefully regulated materials.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** Cutting a support tree can close a route, damage neighboring roots and change water flow around the settlement.

### PLC-012 — Thornwatch Frontier
**Terrain:** A palisaded border town where cultivated land meets dangerous-beast habitat.  
**Layout:** Watch platforms, livestock yards, outer gardens, alarm trails and evacuation lanes form rings around the oldest settlement.  
**Visual identity:** Thorn-patterned leather, weathered green, rough timber and red warning flags.  
**Movement:** Gates and livestock barriers can bottleneck evacuation; sightlines are deliberately kept clear around approaches.  
**Sound/light:** Bells and horn signals have defined ranges; forest noise can mask them.  
**Economy/politics:** Hunting, tracking, hide processing, field medicine and frontier patrols.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** Overhunting changes beast migration; a cleared forest strip may improve visibility while increasing erosion and wind exposure.

### PLC-013 — The Glass Fen
**Terrain:** A marsh where silica-rich water leaves translucent mineral crusts on reeds and stones.  
**Layout:** Raised walkways and floating platforms connect dry islands; old paths vanish after storms.  
**Visual identity:** Smoke-green water, silver crust, black reed beds and low amber lamps.  
**Movement:** Load distribution, hidden channels, fog and unstable ground make straight-line travel dangerous.  
**Sound/light:** Fog scatters lantern light; footsteps and splashes reveal movement but distort direction.  
**Economy/politics:** Mineral collection, reeds, medicinal plants and guide services.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A broken walkway strands people, redirects traffic and exposes fragile habitats to trampling.

### PLC-014 — Emberleaf Basin
**Terrain:** A dry woodland basin with seasonal fires and mineral-rich ash soils.  
**Layout:** Settlements occupy rocky breaks and maintained fire lanes; orchards and fuel plots are separated from dense woodland.  
**Visual identity:** Burnt orange, charcoal, dusty olive, pale ash and red stone.  
**Movement:** Smoke, heat, wind shifts and ember fall change routes. Firebreaks are maintained infrastructure.  
**Sound/light:** Smoke reduces visibility and turns sunlight red; burning timber can collapse across a road.  
**Economy/politics:** Resin, hardy crops, charcoal, controlled burning and fire-resistant craft.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A badly timed burn can destroy a storehouse, change runoff and push wildlife into farms.

### PLC-015 — The Mothglass Conservatory
**Terrain:** A walled research settlement built around a cave system that supports luminous insects and delicate fungal ecologies.  
**Layout:** Separate light-controlled chambers, quarantine locks, water channels and visitor galleries prevent cross-contamination.  
**Visual identity:** Violet-black stone, pale glass, moth-wing patterns and carefully shielded lights.  
**Movement:** Light levels and door protocols matter; careless entry can disrupt species behavior.  
**Sound/light:** Luminous insects provide low light but not reliable illumination for detailed work.  
**Economy/politics:** Medicine research, dyes, ecological study and specialist cultivation.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A broken quarantine barrier can spread disease or invasive organisms; excessive lighting changes the ecology.

## D. Coasts, islands and underwater settlements

### PLC-016 — Aokiri Strait
**Terrain:** A narrow sea between volcanic headlands and multiple current-swept islands.  
**Layout:** Ports face different wind conditions; navigation towers occupy rock promontories, and sheltered anchorages are scarce.  
**Visual identity:** Basalt, salt-white stone, storm teal and bronze patina.  
**Movement:** Tides, currents, visibility and vessel draft control routes. A direct line across the water may be impassable to a particular ship.  
**Sound/light:** Fog swallows distant landmarks; signal lamps require line of sight.  
**Economy/politics:** Pilots, ship repair, fishing, salvage and passage tolls.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A false beacon can cause wrecks, change insurance rates and trigger diplomatic retaliation.

### PLC-017 — Nine-Keel Harbor
**Terrain:** A protected volcanic harbor with deep-water docks and shipbuilding yards.  
**Layout:** Separate basins handle repair, cargo, military vessels and quarantine. Crane capacity and dry-dock schedules govern throughput.  
**Visual identity:** Dark basalt, lacquer red, salt-white dry docks and smoke-stained roofs.  
**Movement:** Cargo congestion, dock closures and ship rigging create moving obstacles.  
**Sound/light:** Foundry smoke and hammer noise vary by wind direction; night work requires fire and lamp discipline.  
**Economy/politics:** Shipbuilding, naval stores, freight and repair contracts.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A timber embargo slows repairs; a fire can spread through rope stores and immobilize ships that are otherwise intact.

### PLC-018 — The Drowned Steps
**Terrain:** A coastal town built around terraces that are submerged during certain tides.  
**Layout:** Upper civic buildings remain dry; lower workshops and shrines use water-tolerant materials and scheduled access.  
**Visual identity:** Sea-green tile, barnacled stone, white rope, oxidized copper and tide stains.  
**Movement:** Routes change with tide and storm surge. Entrances may be underwater, and currents can trap the unwary.  
**Sound/light:** Underwater sound travels but direction is difficult to judge; refracted light shifts across lower walls.  
**Economy/politics:** Diving, salvage, shell craft, tide farming and flood records.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** Rising water forces relocation, changes property rights and exposes buried structures during unusual erosion.

### PLC-019 — Nacre Deep
**Terrain:** An underwater settlement built around a natural reef shelf and engineered pressure chambers.  
**Layout:** Habitable zones are divided by depth and pressure; air locks, buoyancy corridors, food gardens and emergency ascent routes are essential.  
**Visual identity:** Pearl-grey ceramics, dark blue stone, bioluminescent markers and matte shell panels.  
**Movement:** Pressure, current, visibility, buoyancy and suit limitations govern movement. Surface dwellers cannot simply walk in.  
**Sound/light:** Sound propagates efficiently but direction can be misleading; light is absorbed and scattered.  
**Economy/politics:** Reef cultivation, salvage, pressure engineering, medicines and specialized trade with surface ports.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A cracked chamber risks flooding, pressure injury and contamination; reef damage affects food and storm protection.  
**Access rule:** Entry requires a credible technology or power method, air/pressure plan and local permission or a reason to bypass it.

### PLC-020 — The Stormglass Isles
**Terrain:** An archipelago of steep volcanic islands with frequent electrical storms.  
**Layout:** Villages occupy lee slopes; high ridges carry signal towers and dangerous research stations.  
**Visual identity:** Black glassy rock, white surf, cobalt roofs and copper lightning conductors.  
**Movement:** Sea state can isolate an island for days; ridge paths become dangerous during storms.  
**Sound/light:** Thunder masks speech; electrical flashes briefly reveal silhouettes and coastline.  
**Economy/politics:** Fishing, volcanic minerals, weather observation and storm-resistant construction.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A destroyed signal tower delays rescue; mining destabilizes a slope and sends debris into a harbor.

## E. Deserts, salt basins and heat-shaped cities

### PLC-021 — Saltglass Wells
**Terrain:** A chain of settlements around deep wells and salt flats.  
**Layout:** Wells, shade courts, cisterns and caravan yards are protected by layered walls; residential quarters cluster by water rights.  
**Visual identity:** Salt-white walls, ochre cloth, blue water seals and dark shade screens.  
**Movement:** Heat, water load, shade and route timing govern travel. A shortcut without water can be worse than a long road.  
**Sound/light:** Bright ground creates glare; wind can erase tracks and carry dust into mechanisms.  
**Economy/politics:** Salt, water rights, caravan services and preservation.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A failed well redistributes population, raises water prices and shifts political power to a neighboring settlement.

### PLC-022 — The Red Mirror
**Terrain:** A red-rock canyon city built along a seasonal river.  
**Layout:** Homes and markets occupy ledges; bridges cross narrow gaps; waterworks sit below protected intakes.  
**Visual identity:** Iron-red cliffs, black shade cloth, pale plaster and mirrored mineral tiles used sparingly.  
**Movement:** Vertical routes, bridge capacity and flash-flood warnings determine access.  
**Sound/light:** Canyon echoes distort distance; reflected light can expose or blind a traveler.  
**Economy/politics:** Stonework, water engineering, mineral trade and canyon transport.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A blocked intake causes water rationing; a damaged bridge divides neighborhoods and changes law enforcement reach.

### PLC-023 — The Moving Archive
**Terrain:** A nomadic convoy-city crossing a semi-arid grassland and salt desert.  
**Layout:** Modular wagons form temporary streets around a protected water train; workshops and sleeping wagons have fixed assembly order.  
**Visual identity:** Dust-blue canvas, ochre wheels, painted route marks and brass water fittings.  
**Movement:** The city itself moves. Wheel failure, weather, animal fatigue and route negotiation affect the entire community.  
**Sound/light:** Dust reduces visibility; night signals coordinate movement across the convoy.  
**Economy/politics:** Trade, repairs, records, transport and mobile schooling.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A delayed convoy changes market supply in multiple towns; a stolen water wagon can force a route change or split the community.

### PLC-024 — The Cinder Basin
**Terrain:** A volcanic basin with warm springs, mineral vents and unstable ground.  
**Layout:** Settlements occupy surveyed ridges; restricted zones surround vents, extraction sites and fragile crust.  
**Visual identity:** Sulfur yellow, basalt black, mineral white and ember-red markers.  
**Movement:** Heat, toxic gases, ground stability and wind determine safe paths.  
**Sound/light:** Steam hides movement; vent noise can mask approaching danger.  
**Economy/politics:** Minerals, geothermal heating, chemical craft and specialist surveys.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A new vent changes air quality and water chemistry; unsafe extraction can collapse a road or expose workers.

## F. Cold regions, tundra and glacial frontiers

### PLC-025 — The Blue Quiet
**Terrain:** A settlement on the edge of a broad glacial lake.  
**Layout:** Buildings are linked by windbreaks; winter routes cross marked ice lanes while summer travel shifts to shore boats.  
**Visual identity:** Ice blue, dark pine, bone-white stone and red emergency flags.  
**Movement:** Ice thickness, cracks, wind chill and load weight constrain crossings.  
**Sound/light:** Ice booms can sound like distant impacts; snow glare and low winter light distort depth.  
**Economy/politics:** Fishing, cold storage, fur, ice engineering and seasonal trade.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A warm period changes the safe crossing map and strands a supply convoy.

### PLC-026 — The Glass Glacier Enclave
**Terrain:** A research and mining settlement beside a glacier with unusual mineral inclusions.  
**Layout:** Heated core buildings sit above drainage channels; tunnels are surveyed repeatedly because ice shifts.  
**Visual identity:** Glacial cyan, iron-grey supports, amber interior light and black survey lines.  
**Movement:** Cold, crevasses, ice movement and equipment failure create route uncertainty.  
**Sound/light:** Blue ice transmits light; cracks and pressure shifts can precede collapse.  
**Economy/politics:** Mineral study, ice storage, specialist tools and hazardous expedition support.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** Extraction alters drainage; a tunnel collapse can redirect meltwater toward a settlement.

### PLC-027 — The Long Night Exchange
**Terrain:** A northern trade settlement whose annual market depends on a short season of reliable land travel.  
**Layout:** Covered streets, communal stores, animal shelters and heated halls cluster around a central exchange.  
**Visual identity:** Deep indigo, soot-black timber, amber windows and patterned wool.  
**Movement:** Weather closures and daylight hours shape market schedules; cold affects equipment and stamina.  
**Sound/light:** Snow muffles footsteps; lantern-lit streets create strong contrast and deep blind corners.  
**Economy/politics:** Preserved food, fur, metal, guide services and seasonal contracts.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A late thaw changes migration routes and can ruin a year's contracts.

## G. Ruins, border cities and unusual built environments

### PLC-028 — The Unfinished Wall
**Terrain:** A vast border fortification abandoned before completion.  
**Layout:** Completed bastions, unfinished foundations, temporary camps and reclaimed sections create a patchwork of controlled and uncontrolled routes.  
**Visual identity:** Pale masonry, rusted cranes, scrub growth and mismatched flags.  
**Movement:** Open foundations create trenches and dead ends; sightlines differ between completed towers and low unfinished stretches.  
**Sound/light:** Wind moves through empty chambers; distant movement is visible across some sectors but hidden by collapsed sections.  
**Economy/politics:** Salvage, border tolls, refugee transit and competing claims.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** Reoccupation displaces informal settlements; demolition changes drainage and opens routes for migration or invasion.

### PLC-029 — The Mirror Quarter
**Terrain:** A dense old city district built around narrow courts and reflective mineral facades.  
**Layout:** Twisting streets, internal courtyards, overhanging upper floors and gated passages produce a complex sightline network.  
**Visual identity:** Polished dark stone, pale shutters, copper mirrors and colored awnings.  
**Movement:** The shortest route may pass through private courtyards; reflections can reveal movement but distort distance.  
**Sound/light:** Hard surfaces create echoes; sunlight bounces into alleys at certain hours.  
**Economy/politics:** Artisans, brokers, performance halls and private archives.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A fire in an enclosed court can spread rapidly; closing a gate divides a neighborhood and changes escape routes.

### PLC-030 — The Quiet Engine District
**Terrain:** A planned industrial city built around a water-and-pressure network.  
**Layout:** Workshops cluster around service corridors; inspection shafts, emergency drains and public maintenance courts are visible parts of the street plan.  
**Visual identity:** Slate, copper, chalk markings, pressure gauges and pale brick.  
**Movement:** Service routes can be faster for trained workers but are restricted and hazardous. Steam, noise and moving machinery affect perception.  
**Sound/light:** Warning bells are coded; steam can obscure line of sight and create burn hazards.  
**Economy/politics:** Hearth Engine manufacture, pumps, lifts, tools and maintenance contracts.  
**Persistent consequence hooks:** A valve failure can flood a corridor, stop production, injure workers and reduce water supply elsewhere on the same network.

## 31. How these places remain distinct during play

A scene must use the location's actual mechanics:
- Route choice depends on terrain, current conditions and access rules.
- Sound travels according to materials, openings, wind, water and distance; it is not a universal alarm system.
- Sightlines depend on elevation, corners, doors, smoke, foliage, glare and lighting.
- Weather affects surfaces, clothing, visibility, equipment and travel time.
- Population and commerce follow water, food, shelter, work and transport.
- Infrastructure has owners, maintenance schedules, failure points and repair materials.
- A route can be physically open but socially restricted.
- A location remembers damage, closures, deaths, shortages, construction and political changes.
- If a place changes, the change propagates only through linked routes, systems, institutions or ecology.
- Procedural expansion may create minor streets, homes, caves and local sites, but may not silently redesign a named landmark.

## 32. Required map schema for every final major location

- Stable place ID and all local names.
- Planetary region, coordinates/grid and neighboring place IDs.
- Elevation, terrain, geology, climate and watershed.
- Map geometry, scale, districts and landmarks.
- Route graph with mode, cost, access restrictions and seasonal conditions.
- Sightline/occlusion map for important encounter areas.
- Acoustic zones and signal ranges.
- Light sources and time-of-day changes.
- Water, food, energy, waste and emergency infrastructure.
- Population, labor, governance, laws and cultural rules.
- Major organizations and their actual local presence.
- Market inputs/outputs and transport dependencies.
- Hazards, wildlife and ecological sensitivity.
- History of construction and major modifications.
- Information channels and expected delay.
- Current persistent state and unresolved uncertainty.
