# 23. Original Imperial Ruler Bridges and State History
Status: original pre-canon expansion. These rulers are NOT manga canon characters. They exist to give the RPG a usable historical model while leaving exact manga dates, borders and unknown facts unresolved. Names and reign order are provisional and can be revised after a source audit.

## How to read this chronology
The manga supports an Empire founded more than a thousand years before the main rebellion, a First Emperor who commissions the 48 Teigu, a major civil war centuries before the story, an attempted Shingu program about 400 years before the story, and a young current Emperor manipulated by Honest. The exact names and reign lengths of most intervening emperors are not securely established in the secondary references consulted. Therefore the rulers below are labeled **ORIGINAL BRIDGE**. Their order is thematic, not a fabricated exact year-by-year calendar.

## Dynasty sequence

### R-01 — First Emperor, personal name unconfirmed
**Status:** manga-associated historical figure; name and details beyond the established account remain unconfirmed.
Founds the Empire and commissions the 48 Teigu as a long-term safeguard. The project draws on specialists, rare materials, Danger Beast attributes and international expertise. His legacy becomes a political myth: later rulers invoke his name to justify both public works and repression.
**Inheritance:** early administrative network, military resources and the Teigu project.
**Unresolved:** personal name, exact birth/death, exact founding year and original borders.

### R-02 — Emperor Vaelor the Surveyor
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
Standardizes route measures and orders the first empire-wide survey of roads, rivers and grain depots. The survey improves trade and taxation but gives central officials a better way to requisition local resources.
**Beneficiaries:** merchants and military quartermasters.
**Costs:** frontier communities lose informal control of tolls.
**Long effect:** regional map traditions persist, though many later copies become outdated.

### R-03 — Empress Ilyra of the Three Ledgers
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
Separates land assessment, military supply and household taxation into distinct records after corruption causes shortages. The reform is administratively sensible but creates three bureaucracies that can conceal contradictions from one another.
**Beneficiaries:** towns with stable records and merchants needing predictable weights.
**Costs:** villages without literate officials face higher effective taxes.
**Long effect:** later reformers repeatedly attempt to reconcile the ledgers.

### R-04 — Emperor Hadrun the Bridge-Maker
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
Builds durable bridges and military roads through several core regions, using contracts that require local maintenance labor. Roads make travel and commerce more reliable, but also accelerate troop movements and extraction.
**Beneficiaries:** market towns and traveling specialists.
**Costs:** districts along strategic routes carry maintenance burdens.
**Long effect:** bridgewright institutions gain political power and a professional ethic of safety.

### R-05 — Emperor Soreth of the Quiet Court
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
Reduces open court faction violence by making appointments dependent on written audits. Powerful families respond by moving corruption into private retainers and provincial offices.
**Beneficiaries:** some administrators and smaller noble houses.
**Costs:** officials become skilled at hiding misconduct rather than ending it.
**Long effect:** the Empire develops a lasting distinction between formal procedure and actual patronage.

### R-06 — Emperor Maedran the Ash Regent
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE; placed as a possible prelude to the major civil war, not its canon-confirmed cause.
A succession crisis fractures the court. Rival claimants rely on regional commanders, merchant credit and provincial elites. The resulting conflict is remembered as the Great Civil War in later accounts, but the exact link between this fictional ruler and the manga-associated war remains a proposed bridge, not canon.
**Consequences:** broken roads, missing archives, displaced households and lost relic custody records.
**Safeguard:** do not state that Maedran caused the canonical loss of nearly half the Teigu as a confirmed fact.

### R-07 — Empress Terenne the Rebuilder
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
Reconstructs granaries, court registries and major roads after the civil war. She funds repairs through emergency levies that remain in place after the emergency ends.
**Beneficiaries:** trade corridors and towns receiving repair crews.
**Costs:** poorer provinces pay for works from which they receive little immediate benefit.
**Long effect:** “temporary” emergency taxes become a recurring instrument of government.

### R-08 — Emperor Othric the Keeper of Seals
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
Centralizes records of rare weapons, dangerous materials and restricted research. The measure is partly motivated by the chaos of missing relics, but also prevents provinces from keeping independent records of what the capital takes.
**Beneficiaries:** central custodians and officials.
**Costs:** local histories and ownership claims disappear into the central archive.
**Long effect:** relic registries become politically important and vulnerable to falsification.

### R-09 — Emperor Narev of the Closed Workshop
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
Restricts certain alchemical and relic-making practices after accidents in state workshops. Some skilled artisans move abroad or into private patronage. The restrictions reduce some risks while encouraging illicit workshops.
**Beneficiaries:** officials worried about uncontrolled weapons.
**Costs:** legitimate research slows; black markets gain specialist knowledge.
**Long effect:** later rulers inherit a state that depends on rare expertise it has made difficult to cultivate.

### R-10 — The Shingu Emperor, personal name unresolved
**Status:** historical episode is secondary-reported; identity and detailed personal history unresolved.
About 400 years before the main story, an emperor attempts to create weapons that surpass the original Teigu. The resulting Shingu are judged inferior and sealed, later being issued to the Elite Seven according to secondary references. This entry must not be merged with the older Great Civil War without evidence.
**Original interpretation:** the program's failure creates a lasting political lesson: rulers want strategic miracles, while technical work obeys material and knowledge limits.
**Long effect:** sealed arsenals, restricted workshops and competing stories about why the program failed.

### R-11 — Emperor Pellian the Provincial Bargainer
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
Restores provincial elites to formal councils in exchange for reliable tax and troop quotas. Some regions gain legal recognition; others are excluded because they lack bargaining leverage.
**Beneficiaries:** recognized provincial houses and merchants seeking stable contracts.
**Costs:** communities outside the recognized system become easier to exploit.
**Long effect:** local autonomy varies widely within the same Empire.

### R-12 — Empress Sarune the River Warden
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
Funds waterworks and river defenses after repeated floods damage settlements and supply routes. The program succeeds where maintenance is funded and fails where local officials falsify inspections.
**Beneficiaries:** downstream towns, irrigators and river merchants.
**Costs:** upstream districts lose land to reservoirs and compulsory labor.
**Long effect:** engineering authority becomes entangled with land rights and political patronage.

### R-13 — Emperor Dalmor the Far-March Prince
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
Expands frontier commands and uses local auxiliaries to avoid overextending core troops. The arrangement is effective but gives frontier commanders local networks that do not always obey the capital.
**Beneficiaries:** frontier towns protected from raids and merchants using guarded routes.
**Costs:** conscription, land seizure and cultural pressure.
**Long effect:** frontier loyalty becomes conditional on supplies, local bargains and credible threats.

### R-14 — Emperor Istran the Ledgerless
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
Attempts to simplify taxes by abolishing several old records and replacing them with a single central assessment. Officials exploit the transition to hide arrears and seize disputed property.
**Beneficiaries:** central collectors and politically connected creditors.
**Costs:** households lose evidence of prior payments.
**Long effect:** later provincial courts preserve unofficial receipts and witness chains.

### R-15 — Emperor Belisar the Patron of Stone
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
Sponsors monumental capital works to project stability after a period of provincial unrest. Some structures become useful infrastructure; others drain funds and require permanent labor.
**Beneficiaries:** capital contractors and districts receiving new water, roads or walls.
**Costs:** distant provinces face levies and labor requisition.
**Long effect:** monumental architecture becomes a propaganda language and a maintenance liability.

### R-16 — Emperor Caldrin the Short-Reigned
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
A brief reign ends before succession rules are clarified. Court factions learn to govern through temporary appointments and private guarantees.
**Beneficiaries:** advisers who can broker between claimants.
**Costs:** long-term projects stall and records diverge.
**Long effect:** the court becomes more vulnerable to a skilled patronage broker.

### R-17 — Empress Ysolda the Cautious
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
Avoids major wars, negotiates several border agreements and reduces some garrison spending. Her policy improves the treasury but leaves certain frontier communities feeling abandoned.
**Beneficiaries:** merchants and taxpayers in stable regions.
**Costs:** border settlements face longer response times.
**Long effect:** the Empire's apparent peace hides uneven protection and resentful local memory.

### R-18 — Emperor Merovan the Heir's Guardian
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
Rules during a young heir's minority or dependency, relying on competing court guardians. The fictional details are intentionally not tied to the current Emperor's immediate parents unless later source verification permits it.
**Beneficiaries:** the court faction that controls access to the heir.
**Costs:** the ruler's public authority weakens when advisers act in the sovereign's name.
**Long effect:** the institution of “protecting” a young monarch becomes a route to controlling information.

### R-19 — Emperor Orsenn the Last Auditor
**Status:** ORIGINAL BRIDGE.
Attempts to expose patronage networks through financial audits. Several honest officials are dismissed or killed; others compromise to survive. The reform fails institutionally but leaves records later used by rebels and provincial litigants.
**Beneficiaries:** investigators and communities seeking proof of extortion.
**Costs:** witnesses are threatened; the audit system loses legitimacy.
**Long effect:** reform documents become dangerous evidence rather than functioning controls.

### R-20 — The Current Emperor's Father, name unresolved
**Status:** manga-associated relationship; personal name and full reign history require source verification.
The young Emperor's father rules before Honest's consolidation of control. Honest secretly poisons him and the Empress, leaving the child as the surviving heir according to character summaries. Do not invent an exact reign length or personal name without checking the manga.
**Institutional condition:** the court is already vulnerable to patronage, violence and information control; Honest exploits these weaknesses.

### R-21 — The Current Emperor
**Status:** manga canon.
A young heir who wants to lead the people toward a better future but lacks the experience and independent access to information needed to govern effectively. Honest manipulates him and conceals the murder of his parents. The Emperor's capacity to operate Shikoutazer makes him a central figure in the manga's final conflict.
**Campaign lock:** preserve the manga's events and ending. Do not rewrite his choices or fate unless the player has deliberately created an alternate-history branch.

## What this sequence does and does not establish
- It provides a usable institutional past: roads, ledgers, provincial bargaining, waterworks, relic custody, frontier administration, succession problems and reform failures.
- It does not claim that the original rulers above appear in the manga.
- It does not assign exact regnal years, birth dates or total emperor counts.
- It does not declare Maedran the confirmed cause of the Great Civil War.
- It does not give the First Emperor a fabricated personal name.
- It keeps the Shingu attempt distinct from the civil war.
- It leaves the current Emperor's parents' names and exact reign details unresolved until source verification.
- The names may be changed to fit future linguistic regions, but any change must be propagated through the history graph.

## Historical event graph
For each ruler, connect:
- inherited institutions;
- reforms and costs;
- beneficiaries and harmed groups;
- resource and route changes;
- documents produced or destroyed;
- factional consequences;
- what later historians remember incorrectly.
A ruler's policy can have multiple outcomes. Do not turn the entire thousand-year history into a single inevitable decline. Periods of competent administration, local prosperity, compromise, resistance and partial recovery should coexist with coercion and corruption.
