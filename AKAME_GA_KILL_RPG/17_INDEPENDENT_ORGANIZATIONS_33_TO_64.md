# 17. Independent Organization Registry: ORG-033 to ORG-064
Status: original RPG expansion, not manga canon. Names are provisional until the world map and language families are locked. Each organization has an independent material basis; none exists merely to serve the Empire.

## Desert, water and caravan powers
### ORG-033 — The Cistern Synod
A federation of desert water engineers who certify reservoirs, wells and flood gates. Power basis: civil engineering, records and mutual rescue contracts, not magic. Visual identity: slate-blue water marks, copper measuring chains and white maintenance coats. Operates across several states without owning a contiguous territory. Limitation: it cannot force compliance where its inspectors lack escorts. Rival: Red Ledger Houses over who controls water-credit records. Failure mode: falsified measurements trigger cascading rationing errors.

### ORG-034 — The Saffron Veil Couriers
A courier network specializing in high-risk desert routes and confidential messages. Power basis: route knowledge, relay shelters, trained animals and compartmentalized information. Aesthetic: layered saffron-and-ash travel wraps with route knots, not uniforms. It refuses to guarantee delivery where water stations are unverified. Rival: Glass Orchard. Failure mode: one compromised relay can spread a false message through several branches before the error is detected.

### ORG-035 — The Salt Widows' Assembly
A cooperative of salt-flat workers, healers and caravan widows who own extraction rights collectively. Power basis: labor solidarity, mineral expertise and mutual aid. Aesthetic: pale workcloth, red mineral markings and heavy ceramic water jars. Operates independently of the White Pilgrimage. Limitation: seasonal extraction and dust illness. Rival: merchants seeking private control of salt routes. Failure mode: a bad lake season can erase its reserve funds.

### ORG-036 — The Thirstless Doctrine
A severe ascetic movement claiming that dependence on water reveals spiritual weakness. It uses dehydration-resistant training and limited Vowcraft, but its members remain biologically vulnerable. Aesthetic: dark layered robes, sealed ceramic flasks, bare stone halls. It recruits among soldiers and pilgrims. Limitation: its doctrine is dangerous and its survival record is exaggerated. Rival: Cistern Synod. Failure mode: leaders conceal casualties to preserve authority.

### ORG-037 — The Dune Court of Measures
A mobile arbitration court that settles disputes over wells, caravan debt, grazing rights and stolen route markers. Power basis: witnesses, portable archives and cross-tribal legitimacy. Its judges carry no standing army. Aesthetic: folding shade canopies, inked sand tables and brass weights. Limitation: judgments fail when parties refuse recognition. Rival: local rulers who resent outside arbitration. Failure mode: forged witness chains discredit a ruling.

## Witch orders, occult schools and anomalous practice
### ORG-038 — The Hollow Moon Collegium
A dispersed witch-school that studies perception, dreams and sensory misdirection. Power basis: trained ritual practice and costly prepared instruments; it cannot read thoughts or rewrite reality at will. Aesthetic: moon-pale lacquer, black thread diagrams and mirrored masks with opaque interiors. Limitation: prepared effects are fragile and often fail under sensory overload. Rival: Black Hush. Failure mode: students mistake induced perception for evidence.

### ORG-039 — The Thornless Covenant
A healing order that treats curse injuries and anomalous contamination using Rootsong and biological craft. It is not universally benevolent; treatment requires consent and payment or service. Aesthetic: living trellis architecture, green-black ceramic and woven splints. Limitation: it cannot resurrect the dead or instantly restore destroyed tissue. Rival: White Briar Hospice over quarantine policy. Failure mode: a healer transports contamination between settlements.

### ORG-040 — The Seven Ash Mothers
A matrilineal order of desert fire-keepers who preserve communal memory through controlled cremation, ash pigments and oath ceremonies. Their supernatural practice can preserve impressions, not complete souls or objective recordings. Aesthetic: charcoal veils, ember-red walls and ash mosaics. Limitation: impressions are fragmentary and can be misinterpreted. Rival: Starless Testament. Failure mode: a politically useful memory fragment becomes accepted history.

### ORG-041 — The Unlit School
A hidden school of anti-ritual investigators trained to detect, interrupt and document supernatural workings. Power basis: counter-ritual theory, mundane traps and sensory discipline. Aesthetic: unpainted wood, chalk grids and practical clothing. Limitation: they cannot counter abilities they do not recognize and are vulnerable to ordinary force. Rival: Ninth Lantern. Failure mode: skepticism causes them to dismiss a real anomaly.

### ORG-042 — The Violet Aperture
A small order that studies void-like spatial anomalies and purple miasma phenomena. It does not control a universal “void element.” Members use sealed chambers, distance markers and sacrificial instruments to observe unstable events. Aesthetic: violet-black glass, pale hazard cords and layered respirators. Limitation: exposure can cause disorientation, memory gaps or physical damage. Rival: Pale Cartographers. Failure mode: an observation site becomes a breach because containment is treated as permanent.

### ORG-043 — The Candleless Choir
A subterranean religious-ritual community whose ceremonies rely on rhythm, air movement and touch rather than light. It may use Salt Memory but has no monopoly over underground peoples. Aesthetic: carved resonant stone, tactile wall scripts and soft grey textiles. Limitation: sound can reveal its location and disrupt ritual coordination. Rival: Iron Psalm. Failure mode: a changed tunnel acoustics makes a ceremony dangerously inaccurate.

### ORG-044 — The Red Thread Anatomists
A guild of physicians, surgeons and curse researchers who map injuries and supernatural effects without assuming every condition has magical origins. Power basis: medical craft, observation and regulated experimental practice. Aesthetic: red thread labels, bone-white aprons and portable instrument cases. Limitation: scarce supplies, ethical conflict and incomplete knowledge. Rival: Dorothea-style illicit alchemy practitioners. Failure mode: institutional prestige hides a failed treatment.

## Underground societies and deep-earth communities
### ORG-045 — The Deep Bell Republics
A league of independent cavern settlements connected by acoustic beacons and negotiated tunnel access. Power basis: route maintenance, ventilation and trade. Aesthetic: bronze resonance plates, soot-blue stone and suspended walkways. Limitation: messages distort between chambers; no single council controls every republic. Rival: Gutter Parliament. Failure mode: a blocked shaft isolates a settlement and creates food scarcity.

### ORG-046 — The Mycelial Kinship
A network of communities living around enormous fungal ecologies. Some are human, some nonhuman, and some mixed; membership is cultural, not one biological race. Power basis: fungal cultivation, medicine and ecological knowledge. Aesthetic: soft amber bioluminescence, woven root-fiber and damp ceramic. Limitation: spores, contamination and slow food cycles. Rival: Deep Ember Caravans over extraction rights. Failure mode: a poorly understood fungus collapses a food web.

### ORG-047 — The Umbral Ferrymen
Independent guides transporting people and goods through flooded caverns and underground rivers. Power basis: current reading, ropes, boats and local agreements. Aesthetic: tarred fabric, pale route tags and waterproof lantern housings. Limitation: flood pulses and unstable passages. Rival: Copper Wake Guild. Failure mode: an upstream collapse changes a familiar river overnight.

### ORG-048 — The Basalt Parliament
A coalition of underground mining towns whose delegates are elected by maintenance crews and households affected by extraction. Power basis: mine safety, ore supply and mutual defense. Aesthetic: black basalt halls, yellow hazard stripes and engraved ventilation maps. Limitation: slow consensus and conflicting local needs. Rival: Kharz industrial houses. Failure mode: a production quota overrides a safety warning.

### ORG-049 — The Mute Burrow
A secretive community adapted to environments where sound carries dangerously far. It uses sign languages, tactile markings and vibration codes. Power basis: stealth, local terrain and careful social trust. Aesthetic: soft layered clothing, matte stone and low doorways. Limitation: outsiders need time to communicate; visual obstructions can be severe. Rival: Lantern Courts. Failure mode: a forged vibration signal triggers a fatal evacuation.

## Species and supernatural communities
### ORG-050 — The Lumen-Molt Assemblies
A political union of sapient beings that shed luminous outer membranes during a life stage. They are not Danger Beasts by default and do not share one temperament. Power basis: ecological knowledge, bioluminescent signaling and specialized crafts. Aesthetic: translucent mineral armor, layered light patterns and dark reflective halls. Limitation: shedding leaves members vulnerable; signals can be spoofed. Rival: collectors who trade their shed material. Failure mode: a market creates incentives for forced molting.

### ORG-051 — The Vey-Silt Houses
A people whose bodies incorporate mineral-rich sediment into protective outer layers. Their settlements follow suitable mud chemistry and seasonal water. Power basis: material craft and local ecology, not universal earth magic. Aesthetic: sculpted clay plates, river-black pigments and layered courtyards. Limitation: unsuitable water or pollutants weaken their outer layers. Rival: Red Silt Mandate over river rights. Failure mode: industrial runoff causes a delayed health crisis.

### ORG-052 — The Miasma-Born Witnesses
A loose network of entities able to survive in certain purple-grey toxic or anomalous vapors. Their origins vary and their “species” classification remains disputed. They exchange information through scent, pressure and visible condensate patterns. Aesthetic: veiled silhouettes, refracted outlines and nonuniform body forms. Limitation: many cannot tolerate open clean air or cross incompatible atmospheres. Rival: Violet Aperture researchers, sometimes allies. Failure mode: outsiders misread a warning signal as aggression.

### ORG-053 — The Glass-Shell Pilots
A maritime community of amphibious sapients with transparent mineralized shell structures. They navigate reefs and underwater caverns but do not breathe underwater without physiological support. Power basis: tidal knowledge, reef engineering and specialist diving. Aesthetic: nacre, dark kelp fiber and angular shell architecture. Limitation: shell fractures, freshwater stress and pressure limits. Rival: Nine-Keel Directorate. Failure mode: a damaged reef makes ancestral routes impassable.

### ORG-054 — The Ash Antler Clans
Several related but politically independent forest-edge peoples with branching keratin growths that change with age and nutrition. They are not one kingdom. Power basis: tracking, forest management and negotiated seasonal access. Aesthetic: bark-dyed textiles, carved horn rings and modular timber homes. Limitation: habitat loss and disease; visible status markers invite stereotyping. Rival: logging syndicates. Failure mode: one clan's retaliation is falsely attributed to all clans.

### ORG-055 — The Pale Tide Courts
A collection of sea-floor and tidal-cavern polities that arbitrate fishing, salvage and passage rights. Power basis: tide prediction, pressure engineering and treaty records. Aesthetic: ceramic towers, shell inlay and drifting light buoys. Limitation: storms, pressure boundaries and slow communication with surface states. Rival: Nine-Keel Directorate. Failure mode: a surface treaty is misunderstood because the translated tide-cycle terms differ.

### ORG-056 — The Unmoored Choir
A community of entities that appear near abandoned settlements, wrecks or failed rituals. It is unresolved whether they share a species, a curse or a social identity. Power basis: memory fragments, salvage and an ability to detect certain abandoned structures. Aesthetic: mismatched relic clothing, low echoing voices and portable memorial objects. Limitation: they cannot reliably identify the cause of the traces they perceive. Rival: Last Archive. Failure mode: mistaken identity sparks violence.

## Independent civic, military and knowledge institutions
### ORG-057 — The Bridgewright Oath
A transnational fellowship that certifies bridges, ferries, retaining walls and flood structures. Power basis: specialist engineering and shared safety standards. Aesthetic: stone-grey coats, red load marks and portable measuring frames. Limitation: cannot compel repair budgets. Rival: warlords who demand unsafe construction. Failure mode: political pressure forces a bridge open before inspection.

### ORG-058 — The Census of Unclaimed Names
A mobile registry documenting displaced people, missing persons and destroyed communities. Power basis: records, witness interviews and protected duplicate archives. Aesthetic: stitched paper cases, neutral grey flags and ink-sealed tablets. Limitation: cannot verify every claim and may be infiltrated. Rival: Ragged Census over authority and methods. Failure mode: an error in identity propagates through land claims and inheritance.

### ORG-059 — The Red Horizon Survey
A professional mapping consortium that surveys terrain, hazards, travel times and water availability. Power basis: instruments, field teams and verified map editions. Aesthetic: red horizon rods, foldable instruments and annotated cloth maps. Limitation: maps expire when routes, weather or political access change. Rival: Pale Cartographers. Failure mode: a copied map omits the date and sends travelers into a closed route.

### ORG-060 — The Quiet Arsenal
A network of independent armorers who refuse mass production of certain destructive weapons but sell repairable mundane equipment. Power basis: craftsmanship, supply relationships and a reputation for honest failure reports. Aesthetic: dark steel, pale wood and visible repair marks. Limitation: restraint can make them less competitive and vulnerable to coercion. Rival: Copper Wake Guild. Failure mode: a member secretly sells restricted designs.

### ORG-061 — The Thousand-Step Pilgrims
A decentralized movement whose members travel between sites of historic disaster, preserving names and repairing neglected roads. Power basis: labor, oral history and route hospitality. Aesthetic: layered travel cords, carved walking staffs and locally adapted clothing. Limitation: slow travel and scarce supplies. Rival: rulers who suppress inconvenient memorials. Failure mode: a route becomes a cover for smuggling, damaging public trust.

### ORG-062 — The Treaty Lanterns
A corps of neutral interpreters and treaty witnesses trained to document wording, custom and translation disputes. Power basis: multilingual skill, archives and mutual recognition. Aesthetic: small shuttered lamps, colored knot codes and practical formal dress. Limitation: neutrality is not protection; interpreters can be coerced. Rival: Sable Audience. Failure mode: a mistranslated obligation triggers a conflict years later.

### ORG-063 — The Last Mile Kitchens
A decentralized food-relief network that operates during sieges, floods, droughts and mass displacement. Power basis: storage, cooking, local volunteers and route intelligence. Aesthetic: portable blackened stoves, marked ration pots and public chalk boards. Limitation: food cannot appear from nothing; relief depends on logistics. Rival: officials who weaponize ration distribution. Failure mode: a relief convoy is diverted and a district starves.

### ORG-064 — The Unfinished Crown
A coalition of former local rulers, heirs, exiles and constitutional theorists who oppose hereditary absolutism but disagree on what should replace it. Power basis: political networks, pamphlets, legal scholarship and some armed supporters. Aesthetic: broken-circlet insignia used differently by each cell. Limitation: no unified chain of command. Rival: Hollow Crown and existing monarchies. Failure mode: rival factions claim the coalition's name to justify incompatible actions.

## Registry-wide rules
- Organization membership, alliances and command reach are not global by default.
- Alliances state terms, duration, benefits and exit conditions.
- A leader's death does not automatically destroy the institution.
- Splinter groups require a cause, resources and a social base.
- Knowledge travels through actual channels and delays.
- Shared aesthetics do not imply shared origin.
- No organization is a universal employer, quest board or explanation for every crime.
- At least one-third of major organizations should have internal disagreements that affect action.
- Organization actions consume money, labor, materials, trust, time and political capital.
