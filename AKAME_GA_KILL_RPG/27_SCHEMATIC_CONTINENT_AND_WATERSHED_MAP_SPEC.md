# 27. Schematic Continent and Watershed Map Specification
Status: map-drafting brief. This defines a drawable first-pass planetary layout without committing exact coordinates. All macro-region names are original planning labels except where manga-canon geography is explicitly identified and separately verified.

## 1. Deliverable and boundary

Create a legible schematic world map before assigning exact coordinates to cities, borders, roads, or individual atlas locations. The map should show:
- planetary outline and chosen projection;
- six major geographic systems from document 26;
- ocean basins, continental shelves, island arcs and major straits;
- approximate latitude bands and seasonal circulation assumptions;
- principal mountain axes, uplands, rifts and broad depressions;
- watershed divides, trunk rivers, inland basins, lakes, deltas and major wetlands;
- desert cores, cold deserts, semi-arid margins, wet windward regions and rain shadows;
- a few strategic route corridors and chokepoints, without drawing every road;
- the Empire's verified manga-era footprint only where evidence supports it, plus a clearly separated provisional original-expansion envelope;
- a legend distinguishing verified canon, locked authorial constraints, original authored geography, and unresolved speculation.

Do not present this schematic as a surveyed map or as a claim that original place names appear in the manga.

## 2. Layout logic

Use a wide planetary projection suited to comparing large landmasses. Because the planet's radius and map scale remain unresolved, the first draft should prioritize topology and relative placement over numerical distance. Avoid a projection that visually exaggerates polar land areas without a note. The map should include a small inset or edge-continuity cue so that viewers understand the world wraps around rather than ending at the map border.

Arrange the six systems as follows. These are relative compositional instructions, not final longitude/latitude assignments:

### M-01 — Imperial Heartland and Inner Marches
Place this as a large, connected landmass in the temperate-to-subtropical latitudes. Give it:
- a broken highland or mountain arc inland;
- at least three drainage basins separated by uplands;
- one broad agricultural basin with a major river network;
- a northern cold-transition corridor;
- western broken terrain with several passes and coastal approaches;
- southern transition zones leading toward the Great Arid Belt.

The manga-era Empire occupies a large but bounded portion of this system. Do not color the whole landmass as Imperial territory by default. Mark the canon-supported core only after direct source verification. Neighboring regions and original polities must remain visible as independent spaces.

### M-02 — Great Arid Belt
Draw this as a broad belt or linked set of arid basins rather than one uniform yellow expanse. It should contain at least:
- one large dune sea;
- one gravel or stone desert;
- one cold or high-elevation desert basin;
- one salt pan or endorheic lake system;
- one volcanic or ash-rich dry province;
- multiple semi-arid margins and seasonal wadi networks;
- two or more mountain rain shadows;
- several separated groundwater discharge zones.

The belt should be wide enough to sustain distinct civilizations with different water regimes, architecture, livelihoods and trade dependencies. It must not be so continuous that every settlement is a caravan stop surrounded by sand.

### M-03 — Monsoon Rim and Green Basins
Place a humid or seasonally humid maritime region on the windward side of a major mountain wall. Draw:
- a coastal plain and estuaries;
- steep wet windward slopes;
- large seasonal rivers;
- one broad floodplain;
- an inland drier basin beyond the mountains;
- wetlands or mangroves only where water and salinity conditions support them.

Its main route constraints should be flood seasons, landslides, forest density, river crossings and coastal storms.

### M-04 — Northern Shield and Cold Interior
Place a broad high-latitude or high-elevation region with cold forests, open cold plains, uplands, lakes and glaciated mountains. Show:
- at least two large lake or river source regions;
- a principal snowmelt drainage system;
- mountain passes that become seasonal chokepoints;
- a transition from forest to open cold biome;
- a coastal or inland corridor where winter conditions materially change travel.

Do not imply that all northern people share one culture or political identity. The region is physically large enough to contain distinct river peoples, forest communities, highland settlements and frontier polities.

### M-05 — Broken Western Lands
Draw a fragmented land system with peninsulas, inland seas or large lakes, a rift or fault-bounded basin, short rivers, highlands and narrow coastal plains. Its strategic structure should include:
- multiple independent watersheds;
- several straits and mountain passes;
- a few harbors protected by geography;
- a volcanic or geothermal province only if consistent with the tectonic sketch;
- no single uninterrupted lowland corridor connecting every major settlement.

This terrain supports political fragmentation and local chokepoint power without requiring every state to be perpetually at war.

### M-06 — Outer Maritime and Deepwater Systems
Use the remaining oceanic spaces to create a distinct maritime system:
- broad continental shelves near some coasts;
- a deep ocean basin;
- one or more island arcs;
- reef or shoal regions in suitable warm shallow waters;
- storm-exposed open-water routes;
- cold-current or upwelling regions if supported by the circulation model;
- several straits that matter to navigation.

Mark underwater civilizations only as provisional habitat zones until their energy, food, pressure, access and construction requirements are defined. A symbol on the map does not solve their ecology.

## 3. Mountain and tectonic sketch

Before rivers are finalized, draw a simple tectonic hypothesis:
- one long collision or compression belt supporting the major mountain wall between the Green Basins and interior drylands;
- one older, eroded upland system in the Imperial Heartland;
- one rift or fragmented geological province in the Broken Western Lands;
- one island arc aligned with a plausible plate boundary in the Outer Maritime system;
- one northern glaciated or uplifted highland region.

These are design hypotheses, not a requirement to simulate real plate tectonics in full detail. They exist to make the relief and hazards causally coherent. If the map later changes the plate layout, update mountain axes, volcanism, mineral provinces, river sources and route chokepoints together.

Do not sprinkle volcanoes, earthquakes and magical mineral deposits independently across the map. Each should have a declared cause or be explicitly supernatural.

## 4. Watershed drawing protocol

Use the following drawing order:
1. Mark high terrain, mountain crests and major divides.
2. Mark inland depressions and likely endorheic basins.
3. Route trunk rivers from plausible highland or wet source regions downhill.
4. Add tributaries only when their gradients and catchments make sense.
5. Mark lakes, floodplains, wetlands, deltas and seasonal channels.
6. Add aquifer recharge/discharge zones as a separate overlay.
7. Mark water-stressed areas, seasonal water gaps and contamination-sensitive downstream corridors.
8. Only then place settlements and engineered canals.

At minimum, the schematic must show:
- one large river basin within or adjacent to the Imperial Heartland;
- one northern snowmelt basin;
- one monsoon-driven seasonal river system;
- one inland endorheic basin in the Great Arid Belt;
- several desert wadis that flow only during episodic runoff;
- one major delta or estuary;
- one river crossing or pass that creates a genuine route bottleneck.

Do not assign the manga's reported Great Canal or any other canon feature to an invented river basin until its placement has been verified against the manga. A reported canal length or capital-area estimate from a fan wiki is not sufficient to determine the map's entire scale.

## 5. Climate overlay

The first climate overlay should be broad, not falsely precise. Show:
- equatorial or warm-humid zones only if the chosen axial model supports them;
- subtropical dry zones and continental interior aridity;
- mountain windward precipitation;
- leeward rain shadows;
- seasonal monsoon-like flow where the ocean-land configuration supports it;
- cold northern zones and elevation-driven alpine zones;
- warm and cold current hypotheses where they affect coastal climate;
- desert margins as transition zones, not hard geometric lines.

Do not lock the calendar, season names, or exact prevailing wind directions until rotation, axial tilt and orbital period are selected. A provisional arrow may be drawn for drafting, but it must be labeled as a hypothesis.

## 6. Route and chokepoint overlay

Draw only strategic routes at this stage:
- major river navigation where technically plausible;
- passes through the principal mountain arcs;
- caravan corridors between reliable water sources;
- coastal shipping lanes and sheltered harbors;
- straits and portage points;
- seasonal northern routes;
- tunnel or underground connections only when geology, construction and ventilation make them plausible.

Every marked route needs a reason to exist and a reason its endpoints are connected. Every major chokepoint needs a bypass assessment: if there is no bypass, identify the consequence of closure; if there is a bypass, identify its additional cost or hazard.

Avoid drawing roads as direct lines between all important cities. Infrastructure follows gradients, water, bridges, labor, political jurisdiction and demand. Existing roads should persist until there is a credible reason they are abandoned, damaged, blocked or replaced.

## 7. Empire and canon annotation rules

Use four distinct map treatments:
- **C — Canon-supported:** geography directly supported by manga evidence or by a clearly labeled secondary report pending panel verification.
- **A — Authorial lock:** original setting constraints fixed by the user, such as the 12.3× surface-area multiplier.
- **O — Original authored:** new landforms, seas, watersheds and regions created for this RPG.
- **U — Unresolved:** speculative placement, uncertain source, or a feature awaiting verification.

Never blend these categories into one seamless legend. A reader must be able to tell whether a label is manga canon, an authorial rule, an original addition, or a provisional hypothesis.

For the manga-era Empire:
- keep the known canon incident in its established continuity;
- do not invent a continent-scale migration or geopolitical event that would contradict the story;
- do not retroactively make distant cultures Imperial subjects;
- do not give canon locations exact coordinates unless sourced;
- do not infer that a fan-reported dimension is reliable without checking what the source actually measures.

## 8. Place-atlas placement pass

After the schematic landforms and drainage are reviewed, place the 60 original atlas seeds from documents 13 and 20. For each location, assign:
- macro-region and landform;
- watershed or coastal system;
- climate/biome;
- water and food basis;
- resource or economic role;
- nearest two settlements or route nodes;
- main access route and seasonal alternative;
- political jurisdiction or explicit contested status;
- one dependency that can change if the place is damaged, isolated or depleted.

Then place the 12 desert civilizations from document 16. Distribute them across distinct environments: dune-sea margins, stone deserts, high cold basins, salt basins, volcanic drylands, aquifer zones, river oases, seasonal grassland margins and coastal arid regions. Do not assign a civilization to a biome merely to fill empty map space.

If a place lacks a coherent physical basis, revise the place concept or leave it unplaced. Do not bend a river, climate or mountain range solely to preserve a weak location idea.

## 9. Drafting and review checklist

The first schematic map is acceptable for review when:
- all six geographic systems are visibly distinct;
- the Great Arid Belt contains multiple desert types and water regimes;
- the main mountain axes produce coherent drainage and rain shadows;
- rivers flow downhill and terminate in plausible outlets or inland basins;
- the map includes ocean routes, straits, shelves and storm-exposed passages;
- northern travel constraints differ meaningfully by season;
- there are both dense settlement corridors and large sparsely settled spaces;
- the Empire is significant but does not consume the entire world;
- canon, authorial locks, original geography and unresolved placements are visually distinguishable;
- no final city coordinates or false distance precision are presented;
- every map element can be traced to a physical, historical, ecological, economic or explicitly supernatural rationale.
