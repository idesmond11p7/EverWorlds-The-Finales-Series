# 14. Planetary Geography and Empire Scale Framework
Status: design foundation. Exact coordinates and borders remain provisional.

## Locked authorial constraints
- Expanded planet surface area: 12.3 times Earth's total surface area. This is an original setting constraint, not Akame ga Kill canon.
- Gravity is somewhat stronger than Earth's, but its exact value is deferred.
- A major planetary region is desert-dominated and supports many distinct civilizations, not one universal desert culture.
- The manga-era Empire incident remains intact. Original worldbuilding cannot rewrite canon events; cross-world contact requires a plausible causal chain.

## Manga geography evidence ledger
Evidence below comes from secondary fan-reference summaries and remains subject to direct manga-panel verification.
- The Empire is a long-lived superpower with vast territory and resources. Its total area is not established in the secondary summaries found.
- The Imperial Capital is commonly reported by fan reference pages as approximately 200,000 km². Treat this as a provisional reported figure, not an unquestionable measurement.
- The Great Canal is reported as over 2,500 km long. Its precise meaning and relationship to the Capital's reported area need source-panel verification.
- The Empire has northern regions, including named locations such as the Shiranami Mountains and Hakurou River.
- The Northern Country / Northern Tribes lie beyond the Empire's northern territory.
- Western lands/countries are associated with conflict and Imperial occupation.
- The Southern Lands are described as far south of the Empire. The Ban Tribe revolt and a campaign against inhabitants described as “other races” are associated with the southern region, but the exact relationship among all campaigns is not fully established.
- The Empire's eastern boundary is described as a coastline facing a great ocean; an Eastern Country lies across the sea.
- Putra is described in secondary summaries as arid and rocky, with flash floods carving ravines; dams and water lines helped establish its civilization.
- The setting contains multiple climates and many Danger Beast species. Danger Beasts are not a synonym for every supernatural entity or sapient species.

## Capital size: arithmetic and uncertainty
Assuming the reported 200,000 km² area and approximating the Capital as a circle:
- Radius = square root of (area divided by pi) ≈ 252.3 km.
- Diameter ≈ 504.6 km.
- Circumference ≈ 1,584 km.
These are consequences of the assumed area, not independent canon facts. The reported 2,500 km canal does not match the circumference of a simple circular boundary at this area. Possible explanations include a non-circular or branched route, a different meaning for canal length, translation/measurement ambiguity, or inconsistent source figures. Do not force these values into a single geometry without evidence.

Fan visual-scaling arguments sometimes infer much smaller dimensions from character height and wall panels. Such calculations are sensitive to perspective, panel composition, assumed camera position, inconsistent scale and what the stated area refers to. Preserve competing models in the evidence log rather than selecting whichever is convenient.
Working policy: keep 200,000 km² as a provisional fan-reported Capital figure; do not infer the Empire's total area from it. The Empire remains geographically vast but not precisely measured.

## Planet-scale design
Using Earth's approximate total surface area of 510 million km², 12.3 times that is about 6.27 billion km². This does not mean 12.3 times Earth's land area, population, or habitable area. Track separately:
- Total surface area and land/ocean fraction.
- Latitude bands, prevailing winds and ocean currents.
- Mountain systems, watersheds, inland seas and drainage basins.
- Climate, biome, soil and ecological productivity.
- Habitable area, population density, transport and information speeds.
Do not multiply every Earth statistic by 12.3 and call the planet complete.

## Provisional macro-regions
These are planning regions, not finalized map coordinates. Physical geography and hydrology must precede exact settlement placement.
1. Empire macro-region: central core; northern frontier and cold uplands; western contested marches; southern borderlands; eastern ocean-facing coast.
2. Dune Ocean: hyper-arid basins, stony deserts, salt flats, seasonal wadis, dune seas, high plateaus, oasis chains and cold deserts.
3. Broken Rainlands: high mountain belts, wet windward slopes and dry leeward basins.
4. Green Deep: humid forests, major rivers, floodplains, swamps and uplands.
5. Ash-and-Glass Interior: volcanic fields, mineral deserts, geothermal settlements and hazardous mineral ecologies.
6. Long Winter Shield: cold continental regions, glaciers, dark forests, ice shelves and seasonal migration routes.
7. Nine Tidal Reaches: archipelagos, storm channels, reefs, maritime states and sea-floor settlements.
8. Understone Web: disconnected cave regions linked by aquifers, lava tubes, faults, mineral veins and engineered routes. It is not one unified underground kingdom.
9. Southern Other-Race territories: preserve the manga-associated Southern Lands as a distinct region until their boundaries and history are better verified.
10. Far Meridian lands: remote continents and frontier regions with long travel and information delays.

## Desert civilizations: required diversity
Every desert society must be built around its water and energy budget:
- Water source: perennial river, fossil aquifer, mountain runoff, seasonal flood, fog capture, deep spring, managed condensation, trade imports or a supernatural source with explicit limits.
- Water governance: clan wells, civic cisterns, temple custody, rotating allotments, canal guilds, military rationing or commons.
- Settlement type: oasis chain, canyon town, buried district, caravan hub, mobile camp, cliff settlement, salt port, subterranean aquifer city or seasonal fair.
- Materials: mud brick, fired ceramic, stone, salt masonry, imported timber, woven shade, glazed tile, bone composite or locally justified fantasy material.
- Thermal strategy: courtyards, wind towers, thick walls, night markets, shaded lanes, earth-sheltered rooms, reflective surfaces and seasonal relocation.
- Economy: drought-resistant crops, herding, grain imports, salt, minerals, insects, underground fungi, inland fishing or tightly bounded magical agriculture.
- Political conflict: upstream/downstream rights, well access, caravan tolls, migration corridors, flood control, pilgrimage routes and dependence on distant food producers.
- Aesthetic identity: silhouette, local materials, pattern grammar, pigments, ornament, clothing physics, light and sound. “Gold and veils” is not an adequate culture.

Minimum desert-culture seeds:
1. Canyon civilization dependent on dams and flash-flood crossings.
2. Fog-harvesting coastal desert city with wind towers and water ledgers.
3. Nomadic confederation with seasonal law and negotiated grazing corridors.
4. Salt-flat pilgrimage city whose white surfaces and night navigation shape architecture.
5. Buried aquifer city where ventilation shafts and water courts define status.
6. Black-stone volcanic desert state built around geothermal industry.
7. Dune-sea caravan league whose power comes from route knowledge rather than one capital.
8. Cold-desert observatory culture with severe night-temperature swings.
Each requires distinct institutions, aesthetics, histories, internal disagreements and neighboring dependencies.

## Geography and procedural placement rules
- Rivers follow watersheds and elevation. They do not appear because a settlement needs one.
- Wadis can remain dry for long periods and become lethal during flash floods.
- Deltas require sediment and suitable gradients. Major aquifers require plausible recharge or an explicitly defined non-natural source.
- Rain shadows, prevailing winds, ocean currents and latitude constrain biome placement.
- Settlements cluster around water, food, transport, defensible terrain, resources or institutions. Explain exceptions.
- Routes follow gradients, passes, water intervals, supply needs and political permissions.
- Borders reflect terrain, settlements, military reach, treaties, language and resource control; they do not automatically trace every river or mountain.
- Procedural generation fills gaps around authored anchors. It cannot relocate, duplicate, erase or rewrite a locked place.
- Stable place IDs and coordinates persist. Geographic changes require a change record and downstream dependency review.
