# 01 — Manga Continuity Dossier: Canon Anchors and Expansion Boundaries

**Status:** Research dossier, first pass. Canon claims below are sourced to publisher metadata and secondary references; direct chapter-by-chapter verification remains open.  
**Continuity rule:** The original *Akame ga Kill!* manga is the controlling continuity for the Empire incident. The anime-original ending is not used to overwrite manga events.  
**Project rule:** Our worldbuilding may greatly exceed the original series, but it cannot retroactively alter the manga's events unless the campaign's own actions plausibly cause a change.

## 1. Source hierarchy

1. The manga itself, including the final chapters and relevant bonus material.
2. Official publisher material and author-approved guidebooks.
3. The connected manga *Akame ga Kill! Zero* for the prequel period, subject to consistency checks.
4. *Hinowa ga Crush!* for its own setting and Akame's later involvement; do not assume every detail maps cleanly onto the main manga without checking.
5. Secondary references and fan-maintained wikis as discovery indexes only.
6. Inference and original worldbuilding, always labeled as such.

A fan wiki can point to a lead; it does not automatically establish canon. Do not turn absent information into a confident fact.

## 2. Publication and continuity anchors

- *Akame ga Kill!* is a completed 15-volume manga by Takahiro and Tetsuya Tashiro. Yen Press's official Vol. 15 listing describes the final conflict involving the Emperor's Supreme Teigu, Tatsumi, Esdeath, Akame and the Revolutionary Army.
- The anime adapts the manga with changes before the final divergence. Multiple secondary guides place the major divergence after the material around manga Chapters 37–38, with anime episodes 20–24 becoming anime-original. The safest continuity practice is to read/verify the manga itself from the start, not splice the anime ending into the manga timeline.
- The manga's Wild Hunt arc (Chapters 44–48, as commonly indexed) is a major omitted/altered component of the anime adaptation. It introduces characters and events that affect later continuity. Do not omit it from the RPG's canon model.
- Manga outcomes differ from the anime for multiple characters and events. Examples commonly documented by chapter references include Run's fate, the treatment of Bols's family, Suzuka's survival status, the Borick mission and the later fates of Kurome, Wave, Mine, Tatsumi and Leone. Each individual claim still requires chapter-level verification before being used in a precise chronology.

## 3. Canon geography: known anchors, incomplete map

The source setting is already geographically varied. Secondary indexes describe a world containing multiple countries and climatic zones, including deserts/arid regions, islands, tundra, mountains, forests, rivers, lakes, canals and Danger Beast habitats. The Empire is the most narratively prominent state, not a complete atlas of the world.

Useful canon leads to verify against manga panels and volumes:
- The Empire and its central Capital.
- The Great Canal, commonly described in secondary references as exceeding 2,500 km and built through mass exploitation over seven years. Treat the exact length and chronology as a source claim until verified in the manga/guidebook.
- Putra, an arid rocky region where flash floods cut ravines and historic water infrastructure made settlement possible.
- Northern, Western, Southern and Eastern regions/countries described by secondary indexes.
- The Southern Lands / Ban Tribe conflict and the operational difficulty posed by jungle terrain and Danger Beasts.
- Wakoku, including the Soukai and Tenrou nations and a wider set of nations embroiled in conflict. Wakoku's Meihou and local terminology for Danger Beasts indicate regional technology/cultural divergence.
- Uninhabited islands and coastal/maritime locations.
- Danger Beast territories and routes, including ecosystems with unusual hazards.

**Unknowns to preserve:** a complete world map; reliable planetary dimensions; total number of continents; exact nation borders and populations; all ocean names; comprehensive climates and watersheds; the full origin and distribution of every extraordinary technology; and whether the manga establishes a canonical name for the planet/world. Do not invent answers and label them canon.

## 4. The Empire: canon-safe baseline

The Empire is the central political power in the main story. Secondary references describe it as founded over a thousand years before the rebellion by the First Emperor, who gathered exceptional minds and resources to create the 48 Teigu to preserve the nation. A major civil war centuries later caused many Teigu to be lost. Shingu were later produced in an effort to create new weapons, but were considered inferior and were sealed away. The Empire's modern decline is associated with systemic corruption, the Prime Minister's domination of the child Emperor, oppression, economic distress and rebellion.

Canon-safe design constraints:
- The First Emperor is a historical founder; the main manga does not provide enough verified material for us to invent a full private biography and call it canon.
- The Empire's proper official name is not established in this dossier. Until primary-source verification proves otherwise, use **the Empire** as the canon label. An expanded historical/endonym may be created for our version, but it must be explicitly marked as an authorial addition and must not replace the recognizable canon term in source-history records.
- The Empire can be the largest or dominant state in our expanded world, but this relative size is an authorial worldbuilding choice, not a fact proven by the manga.
- Its borders, military, taxation, road/canal systems and regional cultures must have real logistics. Do not make its reach limitless.
- The manga's revolution and postwar reconstruction remain part of the Empire's own history. The expanded world does not interrupt or erase that sequence.

## 5. Danger Beasts and regional technologies

Danger Beasts are a core ecological and material feature of the setting. Secondary references describe them as varied dangerous organisms, with graded threat classes, rare powerful specimens, artificial modification, human/Beast hybrids, hunting traditions and body parts used in exceptional equipment. Different societies may exploit them in different ways. Wakoku's Meihou are an example of regional craft traditions using such creatures.

Worldbuilding implications:
- Treat a Danger Beast as an organism/ecological actor, not a loot container with legs.
- Its anatomy, habitat, food web, reproduction, territorial behavior, migration, injury, and material yields should agree with the region.
- Mining, harvesting, butchering, refining, crafting and selling its parts are distinct operations with different tools, risks, labor, waste and consequences.
- A carcass can attract scavengers, spread disease, contaminate water, trigger territorial retaliation, or draw rival hunters. Resource extraction changes the world.
- The manga's established Danger Beast terminology and known examples must remain distinct from new species. Additional species are expansion, not canon.

## 6. Canon event isolation rule

The Empire incident is a defined historical event-chain in the manga continuity. The new world is larger than the source's visible focus, but its external powers do not casually arrive to replace the manga's causes, cast or outcome.

- Events in the Empire proceed according to manga chronology unless the player causes a plausible divergence.
- Distant nations, cults, species, realms and technologies can exist without already knowing about every Imperial event.
- External influence requires a mechanism: travel, trade, espionage, migration, diplomatic contact, a shared institution, a physical crossing, or a player-caused bridge.
- No omniscient global faction, prophecy or cosmic emergency automatically turns the Empire incident into a side plot.
- Indirect consequences are allowed and required when causal paths support them. They are not an excuse for arbitrary butterfly effects.
- If the player changes a major historical event, record the causal divergence and propagate only consequences with a plausible path.

## 7. Research queue

1. Read/verify the main manga from Chapters 1–78, recording chapter/volume references for every canon claim used by the engine.
2. Extract a chapter-linked event chronology, especially the Wild Hunt arc and manga-specific final events.
3. Verify the Empire's founding, First Emperor, civil war, Teigu and Shingu history.
4. Verify the Great Canal, Putra, regional labels, southern conflict, islands, and all named geographic features.
5. Verify Wakoku's geography, 22-nation claim, named states, Meihou and the relation to the main manga.
6. Build a canon entity register: characters, factions, places, artifacts, species, events, dates, statuses and source references.
7. Only then grade each proposed integration point as canon, supported inference, expansion, or unknown.

## 8. Research honesty

This dossier is not a substitute for owning/reading the manga. Web research currently supplies official publisher metadata and secondary reference leads. Claims marked for verification must not be upgraded to confirmed canon simply because multiple fan pages repeat them.
