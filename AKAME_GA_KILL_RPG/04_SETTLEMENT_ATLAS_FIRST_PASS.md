# 04 — Settlement Atlas: First Authored Sites

**Status:** Original expansion draft. Names and designs below are proposals, not manga canon. The Empire's actual canon locations must be placed only after source verification.

This document establishes the quality bar for authored locations: each major place needs a reason to exist, an identifiable silhouette, coherent materials, a readable spatial plan, water/food/waste logistics, districts, movement routes, power structures and persistent local history.

## 1. The Crown Basin — three settlements around one river system

### 1.1 Veycross, the Ledger Bridge
**Role:** A major market city at the junction of a navigable river and a long-distance road crossing.

**Terrain and silhouette:** The river narrows between two shelves of dark stone. A high stone bridge crosses the main channel; a lower chain of flood arches and ferry landings handles seasonal overflow. The old town climbs the western shelf while warehouses and newer neighborhoods spread along the eastern floodplain behind embankments. From a distance, the bridge towers and uneven roofline define the city.

**Palette:** River-slate, limestone dust, oxidized copper, tar-black timber, faded blue awnings, wheat gold in the surrounding fields. After rain, stone darkens and cart tracks turn the lower streets brown.

**Street grammar:** Three systems overlap: a pre-bridge winding market quarter; straight customs and warehouse lanes aligned to the river; newer residential lanes following the safe edge of the floodplain. The resulting street plan has bottlenecks where old roads meet new customs gates.

**Districts:**
- **Old Span:** bridge tollhouses, money changers, courts and crowded upper floors.
- **The Ropewalk:** rope-makers, sailcloth, cart repair, timber yards and noisy workshops.
- **Low Ledger:** warehouses, counting houses, public scales and flood-prone workers' housing.
- **West Steps:** hillside homes, water stairs, shrines and narrow alleys.
- **The Silt Ward:** cheap lodging, ferries, seasonal labor and disputed land titles.

**Infrastructure:** River intake upstream; settling basins; public cisterns; latrines and drains routed away from drinking-water access; flood marks carved into bridge piers; controlled grain stores. The bridge is vital enough that repair crews and spare stone are a permanent civic budget item.

**Power:** The bridge council claims neutrality. Warehouse syndicates, ferry families, customs officers and grain lenders compete to control access. No single faction controls all information.

**Persistent hazards:** Flooding, bridge congestion, fires in rope/tar yards, contaminated wells after high water, toll disputes and a structural crack that can worsen if heavy traffic continues.

**Story utility:** Smuggling, refugee movement, information delay, labor conflict, infrastructure sabotage and consequences that propagate along the river.

### 1.2 Cindervale, the Furnace Terrace
**Role:** A mining and metalworking city in a foothill basin.

**Terrain and silhouette:** The city follows a stepped valley below exposed iron-bearing ridges. Mine entrances sit above the main residential watershed. Smelters occupy a downwind bench; slag terraces and charcoal depots form a dark outer ring. A covered aqueduct crosses the valley on squat arches.

**Palette:** Rust-red earth, charcoal, iron scale, pale quarry stone, furnace orange after dusk, grey-green scrub. Snow or rain makes the red slopes slick and unstable.

**Street grammar:** Switchback haul roads connect mine levels to ore yards. Workers' districts are compact and dense near repair shops; the administrative quarter occupies the safest terrace; wealthy houses sit above smoke and flood risk. Service tunnels do not magically connect every district.

**Districts:** Upper Works, Old Shafts, Bellows Row, Slag Steps, Water Court, Charcoal Gate, and the abandoned Lower Cut.

**Infrastructure:** Ore lifts, drainage adits, ventilation shafts, settling ponds, smelter chimneys, water rights and fuel depots. Mine drainage can carry metals into downstream fields; officials dispute responsibility.

**Power:** A furnace council, mine-owner houses, skilled-worker fraternities, debt brokers and a small public engineering office. The city needs imported food and fuel in poor harvest years.

**Hazards:** Cave-ins, dust disease, gas pockets, flash floods in adits, fires, slag collapse and labor stoppages. Closing a shaft changes household income and the city's metal supply.

**Story utility:** Crafting, industrial intrigue, labor politics, sabotage, extraction ethics and evidence hidden in old workings.

### 1.3 Brackenwatch, the Forest Gate
**Role:** A frontier town where a maintained road enters a Danger Beast-rich forest.

**Terrain and silhouette:** Built on a dry ridge above two converging streams. The palisade is irregular because it incorporates old stone walls and later extensions. Watch platforms rise above a canopy line; outside the gate, fenced clearings and charcoal pits form a patchwork.

**Palette:** Moss, wet bark, grey rain, pale wood, old iron, lantern amber and occasional red warning cloth. In dry season, dust coats the gate; in wet season, the lower road becomes a chain of mud and standing water.

**Street grammar:** One main haul road, a market square near the gate, narrow side lanes, fenced yards and elevated walkways over drainage ditches. The settlement expands along the ridge rather than into low ground.

**Districts:** Gate Market, Trackers' Yard, Old Palisade, Streamside, the Sawpit, and the Quiet Row of memorial stones.

**Infrastructure:** Watch bells, maintained ditches, animal pens, a quarantine shed, a public drying hall, ranger shelters and a road-clearing crew. The forest road closes when migration or storms make passage too dangerous.

**Power:** Town reeve, trackers' cooperative, timber merchants, local shrine keepers and the road wardens. They disagree over whether a dangerous forest edge should be pushed back or preserved.

**Hazards:** Night incursions, treefall, water contamination, false alarms, road ambushes and overhunting near the palisade.

**Story utility:** Tracking, gathering, escort work, ecological consequences and the difference between a map route and a route safe today.

## 2. The Sable Meridian — two old cities

### 2.1 Ors Vey, the City of Seven Rains
**Role:** A river-league capital whose districts are organized around flood control.

**Terrain and silhouette:** A broad stone city occupies both banks of a braided river. Seven major embankments, each built in a different era, form concentric defensive and water-management lines. The oldest district stands on a natural limestone rise; later neighborhoods extend onto engineered islands and levees.

**Palette:** Pale limestone, green-grey river water, copper roofs, plum-colored banners, blackened flood gates and bright chalk flood-level marks.

**Street grammar:** Radial routes run from the old rise to the embankments; cross streets follow levee crests. Bridges have distinct load limits and closure rules. The water-facing ground floor of many buildings is designed to be repaired after floods.

**Districts:** First Rise, Charter Hall, the Seven Gates, Wet Market, Scribes' Arcade, the Outer Levee and the Borrowed Banks.

**Power:** The Veyr Concord's assembly is powerful only while upstream cities honor shared water obligations. Each district preserves its own flood records and blames rivals for historic failures.

**Hazards:** Deliberate gate tampering, levee fatigue, disease after floods, disputed water taxes and the political weaponization of engineering reports.

**Visual signature:** At high water, the city looks like stacked bands of pale stone and dark water; at low water, exposed pilings and mud reveal how much of it is artificial.

### 2.2 Kestrelhold, the Pass Fortress
**Role:** A mountain city controlling the safest all-season pass through a major range.

**Terrain and silhouette:** A narrow saddle between two ridges supports a fortress, switchback road, cable hoists and a dense lower town. The upper citadel is cut into the ridge rather than perched implausibly on a needle peak. Snow sheds, avalanche barriers and retaining walls dominate the approach.

**Palette:** Cold granite, pine black, white limewash, rust-red roof tile, smoke blue and yellow warning paint.

**Street grammar:** No broad radial plaza. Streets follow contours and are interrupted by stair flights, retaining walls, cargo ramps and covered passages. Heavy carts use the lower haul road; pedestrians use protected upper lanes.

**Districts:** Pass Gate, Hoist Yard, Winter Stores, Lower Switchbacks, the Old Garrison and the Windward Steps.

**Power:** The Rimeward Holds appoint a pass marshal, but miners, muleteers and winter-shelter keepers control the practical knowledge that makes the city function.

**Hazards:** Avalanche, rockfall, blocked roads, frozen pipes, hoist failure, food shortages and toll riots. A closed pass redirects trade and military movement for weeks.

## 3. The Verdant Furnace — river and delta cities

### 3.1 Namaru, the City on Pilings
**Role:** A river compact's largest floodplain market.

**Terrain and silhouette:** The permanent core rests on a natural ridge. Seasonal districts extend on timber-and-stone pilings above flood channels; broad public stairs descend to docks. From the river, the skyline is a sequence of steep roofs, water towers, granaries and elevated walkways, not a uniform fantasy canopy.

**Palette:** Wet jade, red clay, black water, sun-bleached timber, woven indigo, brass and white river mist.

**Street grammar:** Boat lanes and footways are separate networks. Raised roads connect durable public buildings; smaller bridges can be removed before floods. Street names refer to water levels and former channels.

**Districts:** High Bank, Grain Court, Pole Market, Netters' Reach, the Old Channel, Flood Archive and the Downstream Yards.

**Power:** Grain keepers, boat pilots, flood engineers, neighborhood councils and the Namaru Compact's delegates. Upstream dam decisions are a constant political fault line.

**Hazards:** Floods, rot, waterborne illness, bridge failure, grain spoilage and upstream sabotage. A blocked channel can starve one district while another remains supplied.

### 3.2 Oru's Reed Throne
**Role:** Delta capital whose legitimacy depends on controlling levees, rice stores and navigable channels.

**Terrain and silhouette:** A low palace mound rises above a web of levees, canals and reed marshes. The palace is less visually dominant than the granaries and sluice towers that keep the city alive. During storms, the skyline is broken by rain curtains and signal poles.

**Palette:** Reed gold, deep green-black water, mud brown, chalk white, lacquer red and storm-violet cloud shadow.

**Street grammar:** Levees are the main streets; secondary alleys are narrow raised walks. Districts can be isolated by sluice closures. Public squares double as dry-season markets and emergency refuge platforms.

**Power:** Royal water office, granary stewards, boat guilds, temple estates and local communities with inherited maintenance duties. The monarchy's public ceremony hides how much it depends on unpaid or underpaid repair labor.

**Hazards:** Levee collapse, salinity intrusion, disease, storm surge, grain theft and forced labor resistance.

## 4. The Pale Expanse — two cold settlements

### 4.1 Hearthfast
**Role:** Seasonal confederacy assembly and winter refuge.

**Terrain and silhouette:** A sheltered river bend beneath low hills. Buildings are low, heavily insulated and clustered around communal halls and granaries. A raised watch platform and smoke columns are visible across the snow plain.

**Palette:** Snow blue, dark spruce, iron-red lichen, smoke grey, amber windows and worn wool colors.

**Street grammar:** Short covered lanes reduce wind exposure; sled paths circle storage buildings; summer routes spread toward grazing and fishing grounds. The town's boundaries change seasonally as people move between shelters.

**Power:** Nine Hearth delegates, storehouse keepers, healers, route guides and temporary war captains. Authority is negotiated through winter survival, not just bloodline.

**Hazards:** Fuel shortage, frozen water, blizzards, food spoilage, roof collapse and conflict over shelter obligations.

### 4.2 Varkesh Deep
**Role:** Mining settlement around a mineral ridge and a chain of engineered tunnels.

**Terrain and silhouette:** Low stone-and-turf roofs sit below the mine mouths. Ventilation stacks, ore sledways and heated communal buildings define the settlement. The mine is divided into separately supported workings; it is not an endless connected dungeon.

**Palette:** Black rock, white frost, lichen red, iron-grey tools, fire amber and muted leather.

**Infrastructure:** Drainage channels, air shafts, timber supports, warning bells, emergency food caches and maps updated after every collapse.

**Power:** Kharad Iron Synod, mining crews, ventilation keepers and food caravans. The group controlling air and safe tunnel access can shut down the mine without drawing a sword.

**Hazards:** Gas, collapse, underground water, cold exposure, ore price shocks and contested waste heaps.

## 5. The Thousand Shoals — port and island settlements

### 5.1 Aokiri Sound
**Role:** Maritime league port at a narrow strait.

**Terrain and silhouette:** A protected harbor lies between volcanic headlands. Lighthouse towers, tide stairs, dry docks and signal poles stand above dark basalt walls. The old town climbs a wind-sheltered slope; the newer shipyard occupies a reclaimed shelf whose retaining walls need constant repair.

**Palette:** Basalt black, storm teal, salt white, oxidized bronze, lacquer red and kelp green.

**Street grammar:** Steep stair lanes connect upper markets to the waterfront. Heavy cargo follows ramps and capstans; passenger alleys are narrower. Streets are oriented to wind and harbor visibility, not a perfect grid.

**Power:** Pilot guilds, lighthouse keepers, shipwrights, harbor courts and customs houses. Navigation information is treated as a strategic public good and a private commodity at once.

**Hazards:** Storm surge, fire in shipyards, grounding, false signal lights, damaged breakwaters and labor disputes over timber.

### 5.2 Silt Crown
**Role:** A delta-island principality where channels and boundaries migrate.

**Terrain and silhouette:** The capital sits on an engineered mound ringed by mudbrick walls, reed beds and tidal gates. The outer city is a moving patchwork of stilt districts and reclaimed land. Maps include dates because a street or dock may vanish after a major flood.

**Palette:** Silt ochre, brine silver, algae green, smoked timber, white shell lime and blue-black evening water.

**Power:** Channel surveyors, dredging crews, salt merchants, land-title courts and the ruling house. Survey records are political weapons because boundaries are physically unstable.

**Hazards:** Channel migration, saltwater intrusion, disease, land subsidence, fraudulent maps and conflict over who pays for dredging.

## 6. Underdeep settlement

### 6.1 Bellwarren
**Role:** A cave-network settlement maintaining a critical ventilation and warning system.

**Terrain and silhouette:** The inhabited core occupies several chambers linked by narrow engineered passages around a subterranean lake. Warm workshops cluster near geothermal seepage, while food chambers use managed fungi, insect protein, preserved surface grain and carefully rationed trade. It is not self-sufficient by magic.

**Palette:** Limestone cream, mineral blue, fungal amber, soot black, wet slate and rare coppery growths.

**Street grammar:** Routes are marked by touch-coded wall ridges, lamp colors and directional airflow. Public corridors are kept clear for evacuation; private passages are not presumed to connect to everything. Sound travels unpredictably around bends.

**Power:** The Deep Bell Compact, ventilation keepers, water stewards, food cultivators and surface traders.

**Hazards:** Air stagnation, flood, fungal contamination, lamp failure, tunnel collapse, smoke and loss of surface supply.

## 7. Site design schema

Every future major settlement file should contain:
1. Stable ID, name, aliases, provenance and region.
2. Coordinates or relative map placement and scale.
3. Terrain, water source, climate and seasonal conditions.
4. Silhouette, color palette, materials and lighting identity.
5. Street grammar, circulation, gates, bridges, vertical levels and choke points.
6. Districts with boundaries and distinct functions.
7. Food, water, waste, energy, construction and transport logistics.
8. Population estimate with uncertainty and settlement growth history.
9. Political institutions, social divisions, local laws and faction presence.
10. Economy, imports, exports, labor and resource constraints.
11. Dangers, emergency procedures and vulnerable infrastructure.
12. Sensory signature: sound, smells, surface feel, weather and visibility.
13. Persistent live state: damage, construction, closures, events, patrols and ownership.
14. Causal hooks and failure conditions.
15. Source status: manga canon / sourced inference / original expansion / generated detail.
