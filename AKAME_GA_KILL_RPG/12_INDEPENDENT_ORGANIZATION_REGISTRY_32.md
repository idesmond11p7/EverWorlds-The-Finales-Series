# 12 — Independent Organization Registry: Thirty-Two Distinct Powers

**Status:** Original expansion. None of these organizations are manga canon. They are not subsidiaries of the Empire, and they do not share a single origin, ideology, power system or chain of command. Their relationships are local, conditional and historically contingent. Names and placements remain provisional until the planet atlas is locked.

## Design rule

An organization is a durable social machine, not a name plus an aesthetic. Each entry defines what it wants, what keeps it alive, what it can actually do, how it gets information, what it fears, and why it cannot simply conquer everything. Organizations may contain factions and splinter groups. Two organizations with similar labels do not necessarily share techniques or metaphysics.

## I. Witch orders and occult institutions

### ORG-001 — The Red Loom
**Identity:** Red-robed covenant of battlefield witches who specialize in blood-temperature, clotting, and the management of living tissue through prepared rites.  
**Doctrine:** A body is a temporary treaty between blood, breath and memory.  
**Power basis:** Original Red Loom rites, not Teigu or Imperial Arms.  
**Aesthetic:** Vermilion thread, hammered copper masks, black wool, stitched banners and portable field shrines.  
**Operations:** Field medicine, battlefield denial, recovery of wounded members and carefully bounded sabotage of biological processes.  
**Limits:** Rites require preparation, proximity and a viable biological target. They cannot casually control every body or restore the dead.  
**Organization:** Traveling lodges answer to local matriarchs; a central Synod can advise but cannot instantly command distant lodges.  
**Rivalries:** Black Hush witches contest their ethics and claim Red Loom rites turn healing into coercion.  
**Failure mode:** Internal scandal over non-consensual treatment, loss of trained midwives, or a plague that overwhelms their methods.

### ORG-002 — The Black Hush
**Identity:** Quiet witch houses focused on silence, sensory misdirection and the concealment of small groups.  
**Doctrine:** Survival depends on controlling what can be noticed, not controlling what is real.  
**Power basis:** Breath discipline, acoustic geometry and prepared hush-wards; no universal invisibility.  
**Aesthetic:** Soot-black veils, matte bone charms, inked lips, felt-lined halls and unlit courtyards.  
**Operations:** Extraction, counter-surveillance, hiding refugees, concealing forbidden archives.  
**Limits:** Wind, crowds, rain on hard roofs, open ground and unfamiliar architecture can defeat their methods. Concealment leaves indirect traces.  
**Organization:** Cell-based houses use compartmentalized knowledge; this protects them from capture but causes mistrust and duplicated work.  
**Rivalries:** Red Loom, city watch agencies and rival houses that sell the same secret.  
**Failure mode:** A captured courier exposes route habits, or one cell's secrecy causes another to walk into a preventable ambush.

### ORG-003 — The Ashen Veil
**Identity:** Funeral witches who manage dangerous death rites, plague burials and contested remains.  
**Doctrine:** The dead must be handled correctly because the living inherit the consequences.  
**Power basis:** Mortuary procedure, salt marks and narrow Grave Arithmetic practices.  
**Aesthetic:** Ash-grey linen, white ceramic masks, charcoal script and bell towers.  
**Operations:** Burial, corpse identification, quarantine, inheritance testimony and the containment of anomalous remains.  
**Limits:** They cannot interrogate every corpse, guarantee truthful spirits or reverse death at will.  
**Organization:** Licensed houses answer to local burial law; illicit practitioners work outside it.  
**Rivalries:** Grave robbers, corrupt courts, religious authorities and families denied access to remains.  
**Failure mode:** A mass-casualty event overwhelms capacity, or a ruler seizes burial records to persecute a minority.

### ORG-004 — The Green Thorn Chapter
**Identity:** Ecological witches who maintain cultivated warning groves and limited plant-signal networks.  
**Doctrine:** A habitat is a relationship that must be tended, not a servant that must obey.  
**Power basis:** Rootsong, species knowledge and cultivated organisms.  
**Aesthetic:** Moss-green coats, seed-glass beads, bark plates and living trellises.  
**Operations:** Forest monitoring, disease detection, boundary mediation and habitat restoration.  
**Limits:** Signals are local, slow and ambiguous; fire, drought, disease and habitat fragmentation break networks.  
**Organization:** Regional groves retain autonomy; each maintains a seed and knowledge archive.  
**Rivalries:** Logging houses, Beastcraft traders and other witches who exploit living networks.  
**Failure mode:** A blight attacks a keystone species, or an assembly uses the Chapter's warning network for political surveillance.

### ORG-005 — The Candle Parliament
**Identity:** A coalition of rival ritual schools that convene only when a crisis threatens all their legal privileges.  
**Doctrine:** No school should be strong enough to define magic for everyone.  
**Power basis:** Multiple incompatible ritual traditions negotiated under shared procedural law.  
**Aesthetic:** Wax seals, colored glass, layered robes and meeting halls designed with separate entrances for each school.  
**Operations:** Licensing, arbitration, banned-practice hearings and exchange of limited technical knowledge.  
**Limits:** It is a parliament, not a unified magical army; votes are slow and members conceal techniques.  
**Organization:** Seats belong to schools, not individuals; schisms can invalidate quorum.  
**Rivalries:** Any ruler seeking a monopoly on supernatural practice, plus member schools seeking dominance.  
**Failure mode:** A crisis requires immediate action while the schools dispute jurisdiction.

### ORG-006 — The Ninth Lantern
**Identity:** Itinerant occult investigators who examine hauntings, false miracles and alleged supernatural incidents.  
**Doctrine:** A frightening claim still requires evidence.  
**Power basis:** Survey tools, Salt Memory, ritual countermeasures and trained observation.  
**Aesthetic:** Brass lanterns, blue-black coats, folding screens and portable chalk grids.  
**Operations:** Investigations, containment, public warnings and exposing fraudulent cults.  
**Limits:** They are not omniscient and may lack authority, funding or safe access.  
**Organization:** Small teams file duplicate reports in separate archives; reports arrive late and can be suppressed.  
**Rivalries:** Fraudulent miracle sellers, secretive cults and authorities who prefer panic to scrutiny.  
**Failure mode:** An investigator mistakes an unfamiliar real phenomenon for fraud, or publishes a warning that triggers a stampede.

### ORG-007 — The White Briar Hospice
**Identity:** A witch-medical order treating curse-like conditions, unusual injuries and dangerous power exhaustion.  
**Doctrine:** Care is owed before judgment, but treatment must not become ownership.  
**Power basis:** Medicine, carefully limited restorative rites and specialized materials.  
**Aesthetic:** White plaster, thorn-shaped ironwork, indigo curtains and herb-drying galleries.  
**Operations:** Treatment, rehabilitation, quarantine and training in safe use of powers.  
**Limits:** No universal cure; treatment can be slow, expensive and harmful when diagnosis is wrong.  
**Organization:** Hospices are locally funded and differ in doctrine; some accept criminals, others do not.  
**Rivalries:** Exploitative healers, military recruiters and states that demand patient registries.  
**Failure mode:** Medicine shortage, forced disclosure of patient identities or a treatment scandal.

### ORG-008 — The Cinder Choir
**Identity:** Fire-ritual communities that maintain kilns, controlled burns and emergency firebreaks.  
**Doctrine:** Fire is neither pure nor evil; it is a force whose consequences must be managed.  
**Power basis:** Hearth Engines, fuel chemistry and limited heat rites.  
**Aesthetic:** Soot-orange veils, ceramic throat guards, clinker beads and blackened stone chapels.  
**Operations:** Kiln maintenance, wildfire response, industrial safety and ceremonial cremation.  
**Limits:** They cannot command all fire; fuel, oxygen, wind and heat transfer govern outcomes.  
**Organization:** Choirs are tied to specific kilns and settlements.  
**Rivalries:** Timber magnates, uncontrolled pyromancers and the Ashen Veil over cremation law.  
**Failure mode:** A kiln explosion, fuel embargo or ritual burn escaping into dry woodland.

## II. Assassins, intelligence and covert networks

### ORG-009 — The Quiet Knives
**Identity:** Contract assassins with strict rules about evidence, collateral and payment.  
**Doctrine:** A contract defines a task, not a license to cause unlimited damage.  
**Power basis:** Training, reconnaissance, tools and a limited collection of regional techniques.  
**Aesthetic:** Grey travel coats, hidden stitching, plain steel and no universal uniform.  
**Operations:** Targeted killings, extraction, counter-assassination and deniable retrieval.  
**Limits:** Requires access, information, time and an exit plan; bad intelligence can ruin a contract.  
**Organization:** Independent crews share arbitration but keep clients and methods compartmentalized.  
**Rivalries:** Lantern Courts, rival killer companies and clients who refuse payment.  
**Failure mode:** A false identity, a witness outside the expected route, or a contract whose political aftermath exceeds the fee.

### ORG-010 — The Glass Orchard
**Identity:** Information brokers who grow influence through records, witnesses, debt and controlled rumor.  
**Doctrine:** Information has value only when its provenance and timing are understood.  
**Power basis:** Human networks, archives, coded trade practices and limited Salt Memory.  
**Aesthetic:** Green glass beads, mirrored counters, pale aprons and market gardens behind locked walls.  
**Operations:** Verification, discreet mediation, intelligence sales and recovery of lost documents.  
**Limits:** They cannot know what no one observed or reported; their records may be biased or outdated.  
**Organization:** Local orchards compete while maintaining a shared dispute registry.  
**Rivalries:** State censors, blackmailers and anyone who destroys records rather than bargaining.  
**Failure mode:** A forged source is accepted, exposing clients and damaging trust across the network.

### ORG-011 — The Black Meridian
**Identity:** Long-distance couriers and route specialists who move people and messages across hostile terrain.  
**Doctrine:** A route is a promise about conditions, not a guarantee of safety.  
**Power basis:** Navigation, local agreements, signal systems and endurance training.  
**Aesthetic:** Dark blue sashes, route-knot cords, weatherproof cases and map tattoos that are not standardized.  
**Operations:** Courier work, evacuation, smuggling, survey and emergency relay.  
**Limits:** Weather, checkpoints, injury, closed passes and lost mounts disrupt schedules.  
**Organization:** Route captains control their own segments; no central office can force a blocked road open.  
**Rivalries:** Toll collectors, bandit groups, rival couriers and rulers who monopolize messages.  
**Failure mode:** A delayed warning arrives after the event it was meant to prevent.

### ORG-012 — The Lantern Courts
**Identity:** Independent investigators and adjudicators who resolve disputes involving covert violence.  
**Doctrine:** A killing must be investigated even when the victim was hated.  
**Power basis:** Witness protection, forensic craft, local law and negotiated safe conduct.  
**Aesthetic:** Amber lamps, open-faced masks, public benches and visible evidence boards.  
**Operations:** Investigation, mediation, witness exchange and contract arbitration.  
**Limits:** Their authority depends on local recognition; they cannot compel every ruler or criminal.  
**Organization:** Courts are temporary panels convened by recognized mediators.  
**Rivalries:** Quiet Knives, corrupt officials and clients who want revenge rather than evidence.  
**Failure mode:** A protected witness is exposed or the court is accused of serving a foreign patron.

### ORG-013 — The Ragged Census
**Identity:** A clandestine network that tracks missing people, forced labor, undocumented migration and identity fraud.  
**Doctrine:** A person should not vanish from public reality merely because a government stops recording them.  
**Power basis:** Testimony, duplicate ledgers, travel routes and mutual-aid houses.  
**Aesthetic:** Patched coats, chalk marks, hand-copied lists and small red door signs.  
**Operations:** Locating missing persons, exposing trafficking and helping people cross borders.  
**Limits:** Records are incomplete, witnesses can lie, and rescue may expose the rescued person's location.  
**Organization:** Cells protect sources but can disagree over whether to publish evidence.  
**Rivalries:** Traffickers, prison contractors and authorities who profit from unregistered labor.  
**Failure mode:** A list is stolen and becomes a target directory.

### ORG-014 — The Sable Audience
**Identity:** Professional impersonators, stagecraft experts and diplomatic decoys.  
**Doctrine:** A convincing appearance is not the same as a convincing history.  
**Power basis:** Voice, costume, social observation, forged documents and rehearsal.  
**Aesthetic:** Reversible garments, lacquer cases, removable insignia and traveling theaters.  
**Operations:** Decoy work, infiltration, safe extraction and counter-propaganda.  
**Limits:** Accent, habit, access, witnesses and missing knowledge expose impersonation.  
**Organization:** Masters train apprentices but never share every client's identity.  
**Rivalries:** Glass Orchard, courts, and political patrons who demand impossible impersonations.  
**Failure mode:** An impersonator knows the face but not the relationships or current facts.

## III. Religious, apocalyptic and ideological movements

### ORG-015 — The Choir of the Last Door
**Identity:** A death-focused religious movement with both hospitable and violent sects.  
**Doctrine:** Every institution must answer for what it leaves behind.  
**Power basis:** Mostly social practice and funerary law; only certain sects use Grave Arithmetic.  
**Aesthetic:** Door-shaped shrines, black bells, ash ribbons and open thresholds.  
**Operations:** Burial, hospice work, prison visitation and in extremist cells, political murder.  
**Limits:** Shared symbols do not imply shared orders; peaceful congregations may be falsely blamed for extremist actions.  
**Organization:** Local congregations interpret doctrine independently.  
**Rivalries:** Ashen Veil over ritual authority, and state authorities over burial records.  
**Failure mode:** An extremist attack causes collective punishment against unrelated congregations.

### ORG-016 — The Sunken Saint Fellowship
**Identity:** Pilgrims and salvage communities that regard drowned settlements as witnesses to human arrogance.  
**Doctrine:** Water preserves some truths and erases others.  
**Power basis:** Diving craft, tide knowledge, underwater salvage and local rites; no universal water control.  
**Aesthetic:** Sea-green cords, ceramic weights, barnacle-patterned masks and bell buoys.  
**Operations:** Salvage, rescue, flood memorials and recovery of submerged archives.  
**Limits:** Pressure, currents, visibility, air supply and decompression-like injury constrain diving.  
**Organization:** Crews swear mutual aid but compete for salvage rights.  
**Rivalries:** Harbor leagues, wreckers and estate owners seeking to suppress old claims.  
**Failure mode:** A salvage dispute leads to a dangerous dive or a false relic sparks a regional conflict.

### ORG-017 — The Open Hand Doctrine
**Identity:** Mutual-aid movement opposed to debt bondage and hereditary labor obligations.  
**Doctrine:** Essential survival should not be held hostage to a person's debt.  
**Power basis:** Cooperative stores, labor strikes, legal advocacy and safe houses.  
**Aesthetic:** Unpainted wood, open palms carved into door lintels, work aprons and communal kitchens.  
**Operations:** Strike support, debt records, emergency food and legal defense.  
**Limits:** It cannot feed every district or defeat every armed landlord; aid can be infiltrated.  
**Organization:** Local assemblies can refuse national directives.  
**Rivalries:** Reed Throne estate holders, debt courts and exploitative guilds.  
**Failure mode:** Food reserves run out during a strike, forcing leaders to choose between compromise and starvation.

### ORG-018 — The Starless Testament
**Identity:** A sect that believes celestial signs are politically manipulated and refuses to grant rulers divine legitimacy from them.  
**Doctrine:** The sky can be observed; authority must still be justified by human acts.  
**Power basis:** Astronomy, calendars, historical comparison and a small tradition of observational rites.  
**Aesthetic:** Ink-blue cloth, blank metal discs, starless ceilings and precise shadow markers.  
**Operations:** Calendar correction, anti-prophecy advocacy and exposing fraudulent omens.  
**Limits:** Rejecting prophecy does not guarantee accurate prediction; observation is limited by clouds, instruments and interpretation.  
**Organization:** Schools compete over measurements and philosophical conclusions.  
**Rivalries:** Royal astrologers, apocalyptic cults and merchants who profit from lucky dates.  
**Failure mode:** A genuine rare celestial event is dismissed or weaponized by a rival.

### ORG-019 — The Hollow Crown
**Identity:** A revolutionary movement seeking to abolish hereditary sovereign offices, but split between constitutionalists and violent purists.  
**Doctrine:** No office should become sacred merely through inheritance.  
**Power basis:** Political organizing, propaganda, sabotage and local militias.  
**Aesthetic:** Empty crown emblems, grey standards, dismantled throne motifs and public assembly circles.  
**Operations:** Strikes, constitutional conventions, underground publishing and occasionally insurgency.  
**Limits:** Removing a ruler does not create food distribution, courts or security automatically.  
**Organization:** Rival chapters disagree on transition, property and use of force.  
**Rivalries:** Monarchies, competing revolutionary groups and opportunists seeking a new throne.  
**Failure mode:** A successful uprising fractures over who controls grain stores and armed forces.

### ORG-020 — The Red Witnesses
**Identity:** Traveling investigators who document massacres, forced displacement and state or faction violence.  
**Doctrine:** Evidence must survive the death or intimidation of its first witness.  
**Power basis:** Oral history, duplicate records, material evidence and witness relocation.  
**Aesthetic:** Red wax tabs, weatherproof folios and plain dark clothing.  
**Operations:** Documentation, memorialization, testimony exchange and legal campaigns.  
**Limits:** They can preserve evidence without guaranteeing justice; false testimony and coercion remain risks.  
**Organization:** Independent record circles share verification protocols.  
**Rivalries:** Perpetrators, propagandists and rival movements seeking exclusive control of a tragedy's narrative.  
**Failure mode:** A record is selectively edited and used to justify new violence.

## IV. Mercantile, industrial and civic organizations

### ORG-021 — The Red Ledger Houses
**Identity:** Banking, insurance and freight houses that finance trade, mines and public works.  
**Doctrine:** Risk can be priced, but never eliminated.  
**Power basis:** Credit, contracts, shipping records and local political leverage.  
**Aesthetic:** Red-bound books, brass weights, counting balconies and guarded archive rooms.  
**Operations:** Loans, insurance, freight finance and debt arbitration.  
**Limits:** Bad harvests, war, fraud and currency instability can bankrupt even powerful houses.  
**Organization:** Rival houses collude when profitable and sabotage one another when markets shift.  
**Rivalries:** Open Hand, smugglers, debtor cities and competing houses.  
**Failure mode:** A chain of defaults freezes credit and leaves useful infrastructure unfinished.

### ORG-022 — The Deep Bell Compact
**Identity:** Engineers, miners and underground settlements maintaining ventilation, water and warning systems.  
**Doctrine:** A tunnel is a shared life-support system.  
**Power basis:** Engineering, signal bells, Hearth Engines and local geological knowledge.  
**Aesthetic:** Bronze bells, chalk-white survey lines, dark stone, pressure doors and numbered support beams.  
**Operations:** Mine safety, rescue, tunnel surveying and underground trade.  
**Limits:** No universal knowledge of every cavern; collapse, gas, water and bad maps remain lethal.  
**Organization:** Each site controls its own safety protocols, with a compact for mutual aid.  
**Rivalries:** Mine owners who cut maintenance costs, smugglers and rival survey guilds.  
**Failure mode:** A warning system is ignored or a rescue shaft intersects an unstable chamber.

### ORG-023 — The Salt Road Factors
**Identity:** Caravan consortium controlling contracts and standards along desert and salt routes.  
**Doctrine:** Reliable water, pack animals and route knowledge are more valuable than flags.  
**Power basis:** Logistics, wells, contracts, guides and storage depots.  
**Aesthetic:** Salt-white cloth, ochre wagons, blue water seals and shade canopies.  
**Operations:** Caravan scheduling, water security, route mediation and cargo insurance.  
**Limits:** Drought, disease, banditry, blocked wells and animal loss can destroy a convoy.  
**Organization:** Independent factors compete but share emergency well obligations.  
**Rivalries:** Toll states, raiders and merchants seeking monopoly.  
**Failure mode:** A poisoned or falsely reported well causes cascading convoy failures.

### ORG-024 — The Copper Wake Guild
**Identity:** Shipwrights, divers, salvage engineers and harbor repair crews.  
**Doctrine:** A ship is a maintained structure, not a symbol of prestige.  
**Power basis:** Craft knowledge, dry docks, tools and salvage rights.  
**Aesthetic:** Copper rivets, tar-dark aprons, chalk hull marks and floating workshops.  
**Operations:** Hull repair, wreck recovery, harbor construction and flood response.  
**Limits:** Materials, weather, dock capacity and skilled labor limit throughput.  
**Organization:** Guild branches negotiate rates and share safety findings.  
**Rivalries:** Nine-Keel Directorate, harbor monopolies and illegal wreckers.  
**Failure mode:** A shortage of timber or skilled caulkers leaves ships unseaworthy.

## V. Mercenary, military and frontier organizations

### ORG-025 — The Hundred Mile Company
**Identity:** Contract soldiers specializing in escort, frontier patrol and evacuation.  
**Doctrine:** A contract must state the route, duration, supply responsibility and rules for civilians.  
**Power basis:** Training, discipline, logistics and regional specialization.  
**Aesthetic:** Dust-brown coats, patched shields, practical insignia and route tallies.  
**Operations:** Convoy escort, garrison reinforcement, disaster evacuation and limited campaign service.  
**Limits:** Pay, food, morale, terrain and contract ambiguity constrain them.  
**Organization:** Companies elect or appoint captains; splintering is common when wages fail.  
**Rivalries:** Rival mercenaries, abusive clients and local militias.  
**Failure mode:** A delayed payment causes desertion at the worst moment.

### ORG-026 — The Thorn March Wardens
**Identity:** Frontier wardens who manage settlements near dangerous fauna and unstable terrain.  
**Doctrine:** Defense begins with warning, habitat knowledge and evacuation, not a heroic last stand.  
**Power basis:** Tracking, barriers, traps, local alliances and field medicine.  
**Aesthetic:** Thorn-patterned leather, weathered green, trail bells and reinforced timber towers.  
**Operations:** Patrols, relocation, warning networks and beast-control operations.  
**Limits:** They cannot hold every frontier simultaneously; false reports exhaust manpower.  
**Organization:** Warden districts are locally funded and often politically neglected.  
**Rivalries:** Settlers who ignore closures, trophy hunters and officials who hide danger statistics.  
**Failure mode:** A warning is suppressed to protect land values and civilians are caught unprepared.

### ORG-027 — The Iron Psalm
**Identity:** A martial brotherhood that combines mechanical engineering, discipline and a severe ethic of responsibility for collateral damage.  
**Doctrine:** Strength is measured by what a force can protect after acting.  
**Power basis:** Armor craft, formation training, Hearth Engine support and a few distinct martial rites.  
**Aesthetic:** Blackened iron, white tally marks, riveted coats and low, resonant bells.  
**Operations:** Engineering defense, breach control, convoy protection and disaster rescue.  
**Limits:** Heavy gear slows movement; equipment needs repair; discipline can become inflexible.  
**Organization:** Chapters train independently but share a repair and casualty code.  
**Rivalries:** Looting armies, industrial profiteers and commanders who demand indiscriminate force.  
**Failure mode:** A rigid doctrine fails to adapt to an unfamiliar threat or terrain.

### ORG-028 — The Pale Cartographers
**Identity:** Surveyors who map roads, watersheds, caves, borders and anomalous terrain.  
**Doctrine:** A map is a claim about what was observed at a time, not reality forever.  
**Power basis:** Measurement, triangulation, local guides and limited environmental sensing tools.  
**Aesthetic:** Chalk-white cases, layered maps, brass instruments and ink-coded uncertainty.  
**Operations:** Mapping, boundary arbitration, expedition planning and hazard updates.  
**Limits:** Maps age; political access is restricted; survey teams can be misled or killed.  
**Organization:** Independent survey houses compete but maintain a shared coordinate convention.  
**Rivalries:** Land speculators, secretive states and smugglers who exploit unmapped routes.  
**Failure mode:** A copied map omits a seasonal river or a destroyed bridge and causes a fatal route choice.

## VI. Criminal, underground and non-state communities

### ORG-029 — The Gutter Parliament
**Identity:** A loose council of neighborhood crews, smugglers, debt collectors and street protectors.  
**Doctrine:** The law does not reach every alley, so the alley creates its own rules.  
**Power basis:** Local intelligence, safe houses, bribery and territorial reputation.  
**Aesthetic:** Painted drain marks, mixed salvage clothing, lantern colors and coded market stalls.  
**Operations:** Protection, smuggling, dispute mediation and black-market distribution.  
**Limits:** No stable monopoly; violence can destroy the commerce that sustains them.  
**Organization:** Delegates negotiate temporary truces; some crews reject the council.  
**Rivalries:** City watch, Ragged Census, competing crews and landlords.  
**Failure mode:** A protection racket becomes so abusive that residents inform on the whole network.

### ORG-030 — The Bone Market
**Identity:** A dangerous but internally varied trade network for animal remains, medical ingredients, rare minerals and illicit relics.  
**Doctrine:** Anything has a price, though not every price is paid in money.  
**Power basis:** Specialized processing, black-market contacts and concealed transport.  
**Aesthetic:** Bone-white tags, waxed canvas, covered stalls and strict rules against open flames.  
**Operations:** Materials brokerage, salvage, rare ingredient trade and illicit antiquities.  
**Limits:** Contamination, counterfeit materials, law enforcement and unstable supply.  
**Organization:** Brokers are independent; trust is enforced through reputation and debt.  
**Rivalries:** Beastcraft regulators, Ashen Veil, Red Ledger Houses and rival brokers.  
**Failure mode:** A contaminated shipment causes deaths and destroys confidence across several markets.

### ORG-031 — The Unmoored
**Identity:** Displaced people, shipwreck survivors, deserters and refugees who maintain mobile mutual-aid camps.  
**Doctrine:** No border should make a human life disposable.  
**Power basis:** Shared skills, mobile workshops, local guides and informal exchange.  
**Aesthetic:** Repaired sails, mismatched banners, portable stoves and hand-painted route symbols.  
**Operations:** Mutual aid, seasonal labor, rescue and migration.  
**Limits:** Vulnerable to disease, weather, exclusion laws and resource shortages.  
**Organization:** Camps govern themselves; alliances shift with routes and local treatment.  
**Rivalries:** Traffickers, xenophobic authorities and exploitative labor brokers.  
**Failure mode:** A host settlement closes access to water during a drought.

### ORG-032 — The Last Archive
**Identity:** Independent archivists who preserve records from destroyed towns, fallen houses and extinct institutions.  
**Doctrine:** Preservation is not endorsement; records must retain provenance and contradiction.  
**Power basis:** Duplicate archives, oral testimony, conservation craft and Salt Memory in some branches.  
**Aesthetic:** Weathered blue cases, stitched catalog tags, fireproof clay vaults and portable shelves.  
**Operations:** Record recovery, genealogy, historical dispute resolution and disaster archives.  
**Limits:** They cannot preserve everything, guarantee authenticity or force anyone to accept a record.  
**Organization:** Archive houses are independent and may disagree on access rules.  
**Rivalries:** Censors, forgers, heirs seeking to erase embarrassing evidence and states seeking monopoly over history.  
**Failure mode:** A forged archive is accepted because its catalog looks more complete than the truth.

## 33. Relationship and interaction rules

- No two organizations become allies or enemies merely because their names share a color, symbol or profession.
- A relationship requires a concrete cause: shared border, contract, trade dependence, doctrine conflict, injury, resource competition, family ties, treaty, historical grievance or mutual threat.
- Alliances specify terms, duration, obligations, enforcement, and what happens when one party fails.
- Rivalries can be asymmetrical: one side may barely know the other exists.
- A splinter group must have a cause, leadership, resources and a recruitment path.
- Destroying a leader does not automatically destroy the organization; succession, institutional redundancy and local autonomy determine what survives.
- No organization has perfect information. Its knowledge comes from people, records, scouts, signals and travel.
- Organizations respond to events only when a plausible information channel reaches a relevant decision-maker.
- The Empire is one major state in the wider world, not the root of every institution and not a universal command center.
- Imperial Arms are not the universal currency or explanation of supernatural power.

## 34. Expansion queue

The registry will expand beyond these 32 entries with:
- independent religious orders and heresies;
- regional witch schools with incompatible methods;
- cults built around specific disasters or local ecologies;
- anti-magic movements and materialist schools;
- trade unions, guilds, city watches and legal networks;
- nomadic peoples, island confederations and underwater polities;
- elf-specific institutions with distinct histories rather than one global elven council;
- regional criminal networks and private intelligence houses;
- organizations created by player-caused historical events, with persistent consequences.
