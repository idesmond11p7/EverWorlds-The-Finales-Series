# 05 — Faction and Institution Registry

**Status:** Original additions unless explicitly marked manga canon. Names are working names. This is a living registry, not a claim that these groups exist in the manga.

## 1. Faction design rule

A faction is not a name, uniform and evil plan. Every durable organization needs a reason to exist, resources, recruitment, internal divisions, operational territory, logistics, rivals, public reputation, information limits, succession rules and failure modes. Organizations act through people who disagree, make mistakes, pursue private interests and operate with incomplete information.

No faction has global omniscience. Shared insignia do not imply shared command. A secret organization still needs couriers, meetings, records, safe houses, money, training, and ways to verify orders.

## 2. Major original organizations

### F-001 — The Choir of the Last Door
**Type:** Funerary, medical and recordkeeping network.  
**Origin:** Emerged after repeated border wars left towns unable to identify the dead or maintain safe burial practices. Its earliest authority came from preventing epidemics and recording names.  
**Public face:** Burial crews, battlefield recovery, plague protocols, identity records and aid to families.  
**Resources:** Mortuary houses, trained healers, carts, sealed ledgers, local burial rights, trusted informants and a network of small chapels.  
**Internal divisions:** The Keepers preserve records; the Lanterns escort bodies across hostile borders; the Quiet Office handles unclaimed identities and is accused of selling anonymity.  
**Conflict:** Governments demand access to records; families demand privacy; criminals seek new identities; investigators need evidence.  
**Power:** A few branches use Grave Arithmetic, but most members are practical clerks, physicians and laborers.  
**Limits:** No instantaneous identity verification across continents. Records can be lost, forged, destroyed or contradictory.  
**Hooks:** A body arrives with a seal from a state that denies the person ever existed; a plague cart disappears; a branch hides war-crime evidence.

### F-002 — The Black Meridian
**Type:** Route maintenance and wayfinding fraternity.  
**Origin:** Formed by pilots, road wardens and shelter keepers after a series of caravan disasters.  
**Public face:** Road markers, emergency shelters, route condition reports, guides and rescue crews.  
**Resources:** Marker stones, map archives, local guides, shelters, signal fires, ferries and maintenance contracts.  
**Internal divisions:** The Open Road faction argues that reliable route information should be public; the Tollkeepers sell exclusive access; the Bound Mile faction refuses to serve armies that target civilians.  
**Limits:** Its map is only as current as the last survey. Members may be bribed, isolated or killed. A marker proves a route existed, not that it is safe now.  
**Hooks:** A bridge marker remains intact while the bridge is gone; two chapters disagree over a route; a faction falsifies hazard reports to redirect trade.

### F-003 — The Red Ledger Houses
**Type:** Credit, debt arbitration and risk underwriting.  
**Origin:** Grew from merchant partnerships that financed bridges, floodworks and long-distance cargo.  
**Public face:** Loans, cargo insurance, reconstruction finance, debt arbitration and currency exchange.  
**Resources:** Contracts, collateral, warehouses, brokers, accountants, couriers and political relationships.  
**Internal divisions:** Expansionists lend to rulers; conservative houses favor infrastructure; the Ash Account secretly buys distressed debt after disasters.  
**Limits:** Credit cannot manufacture food or erase a failed investment. Houses default, sue one another, misprice risks and lose political protection.  
**Hooks:** A village owes three lenders for the same dam; a war bond becomes worthless; a debtor's records are accurate but legally unenforceable.

### F-004 — The Hollow Saint Procession
**Type:** Mobile relief, tribunal and religious movement.  
**Origin:** Began as a convoy carrying medicine and grain to settlements abandoned during a civil war.  
**Public face:** Food distribution, mediation, temporary clinics, refuge and traveling courts.  
**Resources:** Portable stores, trained healers, wagons, local donors, a recognizable ritual calendar and a small security corps.  
**Internal divisions:** The Mercy Line prioritizes relief; the Witness Line records state crimes; the Crowned Branch seeks political power and manipulates access to aid.  
**Limits:** Supply capacity is finite; roads can close; local leaders can reject its authority; its reputation differs by region.  
**Hooks:** A procession must choose between saving a bridge town and protecting medicine for a distant outbreak; rival branches issue contradictory orders.

### F-005 — The Glass Orchard
**Type:** Seed preservation, medicine, poisons and covert horticulture.  
**Origin:** A loose network of gardeners and physicians formed to preserve rare crops after repeated blights. Some cells later turned their botanical expertise toward espionage and assassination.  
**Public face:** Seed banks, medicinal gardens, grafting, blight research and crop recovery.  
**Resources:** Living specimens, controlled greenhouses, plant specialists, coded catalogues, couriers and regional growers.  
**Internal divisions:** The Keepers preserve biodiversity; the Graft faction develops dangerous hybrids; the Bitter Branch sells toxins and sabotage.  
**Limits:** Plants require time, suitable conditions and supply chains. A poison needs delivery and a plausible dose. Destroying a garden can erase years of work.  
**Hooks:** A blight-resistant crop spreads with an unexpected ecological cost; a seed shipment is mislabeled; an assassination is blamed on a disease.

### F-006 — The Cartographers' Wake
**Type:** Survey, exploration and ruin documentation society.  
**Origin:** Founded by river pilots, miners, border surveyors and mapmakers who needed shared standards for uncertain terrain.  
**Public face:** Maps, hazard ratings, survey markers, route updates and expedition records.  
**Resources:** Instruments, trained survey teams, field journals, archives, sponsor contracts and local informants.  
**Internal divisions:** The Open Atlas group publishes freely; the Sealed Chart group withholds dangerous discoveries; the Crown Survey sells exclusive maps to states.  
**Limits:** Maps are dated observations, not truth forever. Surveyors can misread terrain, be misled or disagree. Unknown areas remain unknown.  
**Hooks:** Two reliable maps disagree because a river changed course; a sponsor suppresses a newly discovered pass; a ruin's coordinate is correct but its entrance is buried.

### F-007 — The Quiet Knives
**Type:** Decentralized assassin schools and contract intermediaries.  
**Origin:** No single founder. Separate schools developed in trade cities, border wars and court politics; later intermediaries established limited cross-school conventions.  
**Public face:** None unified. Some schools are folklore; others operate under legitimate occupations.  
**Resources:** Individual training, safe houses, forged papers, intermediaries, poisons, tools, local intelligence and escape routes.  
**Internal divisions:** Witnessed-contract schools require corroboration and limit collateral; Crownless schools accept political work; the Severance faction targets other assassins who violate their code.  
**Limits:** Information is fallible. Payment can fail. Targets move. Witnesses survive. A contract can be a trap. No member automatically knows another school's operations.  
**Hooks:** A contract uses a dead intermediary's seal; the target is protected by ordinary logistics rather than a superpower; an assassination succeeds physically but creates an uncontrollable political backlash.

### F-008 — The Deep Bell Compact
**Type:** Underdeep infrastructure alliance.  
**Origin:** Cave settlements linked their warning systems after a flood and ventilation failure killed several communities.  
**Public face:** Maintaining airflow, flood gates, emergency signals, safe passages and rescue stores.  
**Resources:** Bell stations, tunnel maps, ventilation crews, water gauges, repair teams and emergency caches.  
**Internal divisions:** Surface Traders want more trade access; the Sealed Chambers faction restricts entry; the Bellwrights insist that infrastructure decisions remain technical and transparent.  
**Limits:** A damaged shaft, blocked route or failed signal can isolate a settlement. Their authority ends where their infrastructure ends.  
**Hooks:** A warning bell rings with no identified cause; a council refuses a costly shaft repair; an enemy targets the ventilation system rather than the guards.

### F-009 — The Ashen Choir
**Type:** Apocalyptic cult with fragmented cells.  
**Origin:** Diverse disaster cults gradually adopted related symbols and texts after a succession of ecological catastrophes. Their supposed unified founding is a later myth.  
**Public face:** Mourning rites, disaster relief, sermons about civilization's fragility.  
**Resources:** Local congregations, coded sermons, safe houses, scavenged supplies and a small number of saboteurs.  
**Internal divisions:** Witnesses document collapse; Unmakers accelerate it; the Quiet Hearth shelters people without supporting violence.  
**Limits:** Cells misunderstand each other, disagree over doctrine and can be infiltrated. A sermon in one city does not command a cell thousands of kilometres away.  
**Hooks:** A harmless branch is blamed for a sabotage it opposed; a cell destroys a dam and causes the very famine it claimed to prevent.

### F-010 — The Unmoored Court
**Type:** Stateless legal, diplomatic and succession network.  
**Origin:** Built by exiles, deposed heirs, treaty scholars and displaced administrators who lost their states but retained records and expertise.  
**Public face:** Arbitration, treaty interpretation, hostage exchanges and legal representation.  
**Resources:** Archives, interpreters, marriage registers, old seals, safe residences and personal connections.  
**Internal divisions:** Restorationists seek to reinstate old dynasties; Charterists want enforceable legal limits on rulers; Brokers monetize disputes.  
**Limits:** A valid claim does not create an army. Documents can be genuine but irrelevant under current law. Legitimacy varies by audience.  
**Hooks:** Three heirs possess plausible evidence; a treaty clause is accurate but unenforceable; a legal victory destabilizes a border.

### F-011 — The Vow Courts
**Type:** Regional institutions regulating Vowcraft and oath disputes.  
**Origin:** Courts formed after vow-bound mercenary contracts produced cascading inheritance and succession crises.  
**Public face:** Witnessed agreements, dispute resolution, certification of trained practitioners and enforcement of narrow oath conditions.  
**Resources:** Witness registries, trained interpreters, local law, sealed records and custodial houses.  
**Internal divisions:** Formalists prioritize literal terms; Equity judges consider coercion; the Unbound movement argues that Vowcraft entrenches elites.  
**Limits:** Courts cannot enforce an oath where they have no jurisdiction or practical power. Witnesses lie. Coercion can invalidate an agreement.  
**Hooks:** A technically valid vow creates an unjust result; a witness is alive but unreachable; a ruler claims jurisdiction the court does not recognize.

### F-012 — The Nine-Keel Directorate
**Type:** Maritime industrial state and shipbuilding consortium.  
**Origin:** Developed around a protected volcanic harbor, scarce ship timber and the need to coordinate dry docks and naval supply.  
**Public face:** Naval protection, ship construction, convoy escort and harbor engineering.  
**Resources:** Shipyards, timber concessions, dockworkers, pilots, sailcloth mills, naval stores and repair facilities.  
**Internal divisions:** Admirals prioritize military hulls; shipwright guilds prioritize durable merchant vessels; island councils resist extraction quotas.  
**Limits:** Fleet size is constrained by timber, skilled labor, maintenance, crew training and dock throughput. Ships cannot be replaced instantly.  
**Hooks:** A fleet is immobilized by a shortage of a mundane component; a timber concession sparks an island revolt; an admirals' faction manipulates convoy risk.

## 3. State and faction relation model

Relationships are not a static good/evil chart. Track them by domain:
- diplomatic recognition;
- trade and credit;
- military cooperation;
- intelligence exchange;
- religious legitimacy;
- territorial conflict;
- resource dependence;
- public reputation;
- personal relationships between named members.

Two factions can trade while fighting a proxy conflict. An alliance can exist at the leadership level while local agents sabotage one another. Relations have a source, date, scope and confidence.

## 4. Canon faction boundary

Manga factions such as Night Raid, the Revolutionary Army, the Empire's institutions, Jaegers, Wild Hunt and established regional groups must be stored in a separate canon registry with chapter evidence. Do not rewrite their membership, motives, history or outcomes by blending them with the original organizations above. Contact is possible only through a plausible campaign mechanism.

## 5. Faction dossier template

Every new faction record must include:
- ID, names, aliases, symbols and source status.
- Founding period and causal origin.
- Actual goals versus public claims.
- Leadership, succession and internal factions.
- Recruitment, training, membership estimates and uncertainty.
- Territory, infrastructure, safe houses and travel network.
- Funding, food, materials, equipment and labor.
- Information channels, delays, blind spots and verification habits.
- Rules, taboos, sanctions and internal disputes.
- Allies, rivals, dependencies and local relationships.
- Current projects, resources committed and bottlenecks.
- Vulnerabilities, failure conditions and plausible reform paths.
- What members know, suspect, believe falsely and do not know.
- How the player can discover, join, oppose, exploit, reform or ignore it.
- What indirect consequences follow if it gains/loses money, territory, members or legitimacy.
