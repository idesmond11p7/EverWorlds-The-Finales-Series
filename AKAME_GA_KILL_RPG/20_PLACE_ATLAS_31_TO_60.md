# 20. Place Atlas: PL-031 to PL-060
Status: original expansion seeds, not manga canon. Coordinates remain provisional until the physical map, climate and watersheds are authored.

## Desert belt
### PL-031 — The Seven Mouths
A canyon delta where seven dry ravines converge during rare floods. Stone sluices and flood markers divide the city into hydraulic wards. A blocked gate protects one district while endangering another. Conflict centers on maintenance labor and downstream rights.

### PL-032 — Saffron Mile
A caravan service town built around shaded courtyards, animal yards and freight repair. Its wealth comes from verified route information and water-credit clearing rather than a palace. Counting halls dominate the skyline.

### PL-033 — Salt Choir Basin
A seasonal salt lake surrounded by low white ridges. Wind through narrow cuts produces tonal sounds interpreted differently by local religious groups. Dust storms erase tracks and damage exposed machinery. Pilgrims and mineral traders compete for access.

### PL-034 — Urum's Vent Crown
The surface district above a buried aquifer city. Vent towers, condensation screens and maintenance wells dominate the skyline. A sealed shaft may be a ventilation route, a pressure hazard or a protected water intake, not automatically a treasure room.

### PL-035 — Ashen Compass
A black-glass volcanic settlement using geothermal channels for kilns and mineral processing. Heat routes divide residential and industrial districts. A failed channel can produce steam, contaminate a cistern or interrupt work.

### PL-036 — The Last Shade
A caravan stop at the last reliable tree-line before a dry crossing. Shade rights are codified in local law. A fallen tree can cause an economic and political crisis.

### PL-037 — Mirrorless Serekh
A monastic satellite city that forbids reflective surfaces. Deep recesses, matte plaster and indirect light shape its architecture. Shelter is available only under local rules; neutrality depends on supply capacity and compliance.

### PL-038 — The Cold Meridian
A high-desert observatory with underground sleeping halls and wind-protected instrument courts. Its star tables guide caravans across featureless regions. Calendar disputes can delay trade and military movement.

### PL-039 — Red Silt Locks
A river-margin industrial town controlling irrigation locks. Neighborhoods are stratified by flood height and canal access. An upstream release changes food prices and may strand boats.

### PL-040 — Bone-Wind Market
A seasonal market where large animal remains accumulate after droughts. Bone, horn and mineralized tissue are traded under provenance rules. Illegal relic hunters operate beside legitimate anatomists; not every skeleton is a Danger Beast.

## Underground and deep-earth places
### PL-041 — Bellfoundry Below
A cavern industrial settlement where resonance plates relay warnings through tunnels. Mining changes acoustic paths, so old signals can become misleading.

### PL-042 — The Wet Ladder
A vertical underground town around a descending aquifer shaft. Lifts, stairs and rope platforms separate districts by elevation. Water pressure, ventilation and rescue access determine where housing is safe.

### PL-043 — Mycelium Reach
A fungal cultivation basin divided into dry, wet and spore-sensitive chambers. Light, airflow and contamination control shape architecture. Food shortages can appear weeks after initial damage.

### PL-044 — The Mute Causeway
A low-vibration tunnel route used by communities avoiding sound-sensitive entities. Layered floors dampen noise. A loud collapse may attract threats from beyond the visible corridor.

### PL-045 — Blackwater Gate
A fortified ferry station on an underground river connecting otherwise separate cave systems. Flood pulses, boat capacity and toll politics govern passage. Closure forces a long detour.

### PL-046 — The Unstruck Vault
A sealed complex for unstable instruments and anomalous materials. Mechanical redundancies and human custodians maintain its locks. Records disagree about the contents; uncertainty is not permission to invent convenient treasure.

## Forest, mountain and wetland places
### PL-047 — Keth's Rain Stair
A cliffside forest settlement connected by root bridges and stone drains. Roofs shed heavy rain; paths change as roots grow. Overharvesting destabilizes bridges and muddies downstream water.

### PL-048 — The Green Underroof
A canopy town suspended between giant trees, with ground-level gardens and elevated freight paths. Each level has distinct light, moisture and access. A broken bridge can isolate entire neighborhoods.

### PL-049 — Glass Fen Station
A wetland research and trading outpost on raised walkways. Reflections distort depth; some pools are shallow and others conceal deep channels. Pollution travels through water before symptoms become obvious.

### PL-050 — Emberleaf Basin
A warm volcanic valley with mineral soils, productive growth and seasonal smoke. Stone firebreaks and distributed water stores protect settlements. Ashfall can benefit one field and damage another.

### PL-051 — Thornwatch's Old Line
An abandoned frontier fortification slowly absorbed by forest. Regrowth blocks old sightlines, while buried drains and collapsed stores remain hazards. Several states claim its history for incompatible political narratives.

### PL-052 — White Switchback Hospice
A mountain rest settlement on a pass constrained by altitude, ice and sudden storms. It maintains rescue caches and a neutral treatment hall, but supplies are finite and mass casualties force triage.

## Maritime and underwater places
### PL-053 — Nine-Keel Drydock
A floating and shore-built shipyard whose slips rise with the tide. Timber, ceramic hull plates and metal fasteners have different supply chains. Repair may be delayed by missing materials or specialists.

### PL-054 — Nacre Deep's Upper Courts
A shallow underwater settlement built around pressure-safe ceramic domes and tidal gardens. Entry depends on physiology, equipment and acclimation. Surface maps cannot fully represent its three-dimensional routes.

### PL-055 — The Drowned Steps
A former coastal city whose lower districts lie underwater after subsidence and sea-level change. Upper streets remain inhabited; lower structures are explored at certain tides. Flooding preserves some objects and destroys others.

### PL-056 — Stormglass Anchorage
A harbor between volcanic islands where storms split around a narrow channel. Local pilots know seasonal currents; outsiders may mistake a temporary calm for safety.

### PL-057 — Blue Quiet Trench
A deep-sea chasm with unusual light absorption and acoustics. It hosts anomalous organisms and disputed reports of structures. Expeditions require pressure-rated equipment and a credible return plan.

### PL-058 — Tidemark Parliament
A coastal treaty city with chambers accessible only at certain tides. Communities measure legal time differently, making translation and deadlines politically consequential.

## City and frontier special cases
### PL-059 — The Unfinished Wall
A vast defensive line abandoned before completion. Some segments protect settlements; others funnel attackers or block trade. Communities reuse its stone while official maps still depict the original plan.

### PL-060 — The Moving Archive
A convoy of archive wagons, boats and temporary repositories that relocates to protect records during war or disaster. It is not omniscient; its location is learned through couriers, and missing volumes remain missing until recovered.

## Placement rules
- No final coordinates before the planetary geography and watersheds exist.
- Each place connects to at least two routes, institutions or neighboring places unless isolation is intentional.
- Each has a water/food basis, material supply chain, local authority and persistent hazards.
- Revisited places retain their state.
- Major locations eventually need authored districts, elevation, sightlines, acoustic zones, entrances, exits, bottlenecks, cover, landmarks and utility routes.
- Visual identity arises from geography, materials, history and use, not merely color names.
- Minor settlements may use regional templates; major cities remain individually authored.
