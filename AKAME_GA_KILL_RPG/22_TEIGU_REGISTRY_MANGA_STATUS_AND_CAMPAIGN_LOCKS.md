## Registry rule
The First Emperor commissioned **48 original Teigu**. The existence of 48 is a canon-associated fact; it does not mean every identity, location, ability, or current status is known. The reference list reports 36 shown and leaves the rest unidentified. The RPG must therefore maintain 48 registry slots, with unresolved slots marked UNKNOWN rather than procedurally inventing new Teigu. Never fill a gap by creating a convenient item during play.

## Rules for Teigu use
- Teigu are not procedurally generated and cannot be fabricated by player declaration.
- The original set has 48 slots; not all are known, extant, obtainable or active.
- A user cannot freely activate two Teigu simultaneously. Secondary reference material describes rare dual-use strain and injury; validate specific exceptions against manga evidence.
- Teigu can have hidden capabilities, but the narrator may reveal only capabilities supported by canon or explicitly authored campaign rules. “Possibly has a secret ability” is not permission to invent one in the middle of combat.
- Some Teigu can self-destruct or be destroyed through exceptional use; destruction is a persistent state change, not a temporary inconvenience.
- Teigu status is timeline-dependent. Maintain a historical registry separately from the current campaign inventory.
- Compatibility and ownership are tracked independently. Possession does not automatically mean successful activation.
- If a claimed item is not in the catalogue or state, resolve the declaration as an attempt to obtain it, not as possession.
- Teigu properties derived from anime-only scenes must be tagged and excluded from manga-canon mechanics unless the manga independently supports them.
- A fan-reported name or status stays secondary-reported until checked against the manga.

## Adjacent artifact traditions
- **Shingu:** a later emperor, about 400 years before the main story according to secondary references, commissions an attempted successor class; these are reported as inferior to Teigu and later sealed. Do not treat them as a synonym for Teigu.
- **Meihou:** Wakoku's Teigu-like artifacts belong to a different manufacturing tradition. Secondary summaries describe them as more reproducible given materials and money; verify individual claims and do not homogenize them with Teigu.
- **Wrigglers:** biological insect-like weapons associated in secondary sources with Oarburgh-linked assassin lineages. They are not automatically Teigu.
- **Original RPG systems:** Vowcraft, Rootsong, Salt Memory, Grave Arithmetic, Hearth Engines and other expansion systems are not Teigu, Shingu or Meihou unless the source explicitly establishes that classification.
