# 25. Power Systems and Technology Matrix
Status: original system framework plus manga-associated artifact traditions. Original systems are not canon Teigu.

## Shared design requirement
Every power system needs a source, access condition, operational rule, cost, failure mode, counterplay, training path, institutional footprint and social consequence. Impressive ability lists without generative rules are not systems.

## Manga-associated traditions
### Teigu / Imperial Arms
The First Emperor commissions 48 original artifacts according to manga-associated accounts. Access requires actual possession and any item-specific conditions. The original process cannot be casually reproduced. The catalogue is closed, status is timeline-dependent, and destruction/loss persists. Never procedurally create a Teigu.

### Shingu
A later emperor's attempt, about 400 years before the story according to secondary references, to surpass Teigu produces a separate artifact tradition. Shingu are reported inferior to Teigu and later sealed before some are issued to the Elite Seven. Maintain a separate registry and confidence labels.

### Meihou
A distinct Wakoku-associated tradition of Teigu-like artifacts. Secondary references describe production as more accessible given the right materials and funds. Keep their manufacture, price and failure profiles separate from Teigu; verify specific claims against franchise sources.

## Original power systems
### PS-001 — Vowcraft
Source: a binding promise witnessed by a person, institution or place with ritual standing. Operation: a narrow effect linked to fulfilling a defined obligation. Cost: breaking the vow triggers a specified penalty; stronger vows reduce flexibility and may require a witness or deposit. Limits: vague promises cannot grant limitless power. Counters: exploit ambiguity, invalidate a witness, create incompatible obligations or wait for expiry. Institutions: oath courts, mercenary contracts and sanctuary vows. Aesthetic: knots, witness marks and sealed clauses. Failure: literal compliance betrays the intended social meaning.

### PS-002 — Rootsong
Source: trained interaction with living networks such as roots, fungal mats and certain symbioses. Operation: transfer signals, sense ecological stress, influence growth or redirect limited biological activity. Cost: time, suitable habitat, nutrients and strain. Limits: cannot command all plants or create biomass from nothing. Counters: habitat loss, fire, contamination and isolation. Institutions: forest assemblies, healers and living-bridge engineers. Failure: a local solution damages a wider food web.

### PS-003 — Salt Memory
Source: mineral matrices retaining incomplete traces of pressure, heat, sound or contact. Operation: trained interpretation of traces using instruments. Cost: sample degradation and specialist tools. Limits: not perfect recording, not soul storage and not automatic playback. Counters: contamination, mixed traces and false context. Institutions: maritime courts, archives and forensic guilds. Failure: a real trace is attributed to the wrong event.

### PS-004 — Grave Arithmetic
Source: ritual calculations using remains, names, burial records and measured exchanges. Operation: estimate unresolved obligations, death patterns or disturbed graves; some practices bind a limited effect to a marked site. Cost: verified records, reagents and social obligations. Limits: cannot resurrect the dead or identify every killer. Counters: missing records, mistaken identity and fraudulent ledgers. Institutions: mortuary courts, plague registries and battlefield custodians. Failure: correct calculation on false input.

### PS-005 — Hearth Engines
Source: engineered devices converting stored heat, pressure, chemical energy or constrained anomalous materials into mechanical work. Operation: repeatable tasks within maintenance and fuel limits. Cost: fuel, parts, skilled repair, heat management and waste. Limits: no perpetual motion. Counters: sabotage, fuel denial, clogged conduits and supply disruption. Institutions: industrial cities, repair guilds and utilities. Failure: operators ignore fatigue damage to maximize output.

### PS-006 — Beastcraft
Source: biological materials and trained handling of specific species. Operation: tools, armor, medicines and limited traits from verified anatomy or chemistry. Cost: hunting risk, preservation, processing time and ecological pressure. Limits: no arbitrary monster-part powers. Counters: degradation, species variation and scarcity. Institutions: hunters, anatomists, tanners and regulated markets. Failure: profitable harvest collapses a population or creates toxic waste.

### PS-007 — Breath Geometry
Source: disciplined breathing, posture and movement that optimize physical output. Operation: improve timing, balance, recovery and controlled bursts within biological limits. Cost: oxygen, fatigue and conditioning. Limits: no energy blasts or limitless strength without another source. Counters: exhaustion, injury, disrupted breathing and poor footing. Institutions: martial schools, rescue crews and labor guilds. Failure: rigid technique fails to adapt to terrain.

### PS-008 — Echo Binding
Source: acoustic resonance in shaped spaces, instruments and materials. Operation: redirect, focus, dampen or encode sound; powerful effects require tuned surfaces or devices. Cost: setup, correct geometry and feedback risk. Limits: not mind control or universal open-air sound control. Counters: irregular surfaces, moving boundaries, competing noise and broken instruments. Institutions: underground republics, signal corps and tunnel engineers. Failure: renovations invalidate an old design.

### PS-009 — Thread Calculus
Source: fine filaments, tension control, knots and anchored mechanical systems. Operation: trip lines, restraints, lifting rigs, signals and mechanical traps. Cost: setup, anchor strength, fatigue and exposure. Limits: lines are not invisible, unbreakable or infinite. Counters: inspection, cutting tools, slack, heat and anchor removal. Institutions: assassins, rescue crews, stage engineers and bridgewrights. Failure: a sound trap is discovered through a different information channel.

### PS-010 — Miasma Acclimation
Source: physiological adaptation, protective equipment and limited symbiosis with a specific anomalous vapor. Operation: reduce exposure or sense one defined miasma type. Cost: maintenance, metabolic strain and contamination risk. Limits: no universal toxin or supernatural immunity. Counters: different chemistry, ventilation, barriers and exposure duration. Institutions: explorers, containment crews and underground settlements. Failure: tolerance to one bloom is mistaken for immunity to another.

## Interaction rules
- Systems may interfere without becoming interchangeable. Rootsong may be disrupted by contamination; Vowcraft binds another practitioner only when the terms and witness conditions are valid.
- No universal anti-magic automatically shuts down every system.
- Cross-system interactions require a stated mechanism and may remain uncertain until tested.
- Access requires training, resources, institutions or unusual circumstances; it is not free.
- Power use has time, resource, visibility, evidence and social consequences.
- Institutions shape training, regulation, taboo and black markets.
- Counterplay must be discoverable through observation or investigation, not arbitrary narrator fiat.
- Unique powers, named relics and plot-critical abilities remain authored entries.

## Required operational card for every ability
Activation; range; duration; cost; failure; counterplay; observable signs; skill progression; social/legal status; known users; source confidence. No ability enters play solely because it sounds impressive.
