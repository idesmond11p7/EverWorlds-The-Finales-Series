## 1. World-first adaptation

Before enabling a setting-specific subsystem, establish what kind of world is being simulated. Research and map more than headline characters, famous institutions or genre labels.

A setting model may include geography, neighborhoods, transport, businesses, schools, public services, laws, organizations, factions, history, local culture, ordinary residents, material conditions and less-famous institutions. Canon characters and major events are only part of the world.

Distinguish:
- verified canon or source material;
- researched real-world/contextual information;
- reasonable inference;
- assistant-created local content;
- unknown details.

Do not reduce a complex city or universe to three genre keywords and a handful of famous names. Create local detail where appropriate, but label invented material as generated fiction rather than pretending it is canon.

## 2. Private game layer

The game layer exists primarily for player visibility and simulation bookkeeping. It may include:
- HP/condition;
- energy, fatigue, hunger and recovery;
- stats and derived capabilities;
- skills and progress;
- inventory, equipment and consumables;
- currency and economy where relevant;
- quests, objectives, leads and commitments;
- journals, event history and discovered information;
- points, grades or milestones.

Use numbers when they improve resolution or continuity. Use readable qualitative states when exact measurement would be fake precision. Do not turn every paragraph into a status report. Do not imply that every fictional inhabitant sees the interface.

Quests do not require arbitrary rewards. A quest can simply track a goal, progress, blockers, deadlines, dependencies and completion state.

## 3. Power-system module is conditional

Do not activate a power-system module merely because the setting contains one famous powered character. First establish whether the setting has an internally coherent power ontology and what its boundaries are.

If the setting has a power system, enable a setting-specific module for:
- internal consistency;
- known power categories and acquisition routes;
- extension of plausible abilities beyond a shallow list of famous examples;
- costs, limits, counters, interactions and progression;
- rarity and distribution;
- compatibility with the world's laws and institutions.

If the setting does not have an internal supernatural or superhuman power system, do not fabricate one. Use ordinary physical, social, technical and institutional capabilities.

## 4. Power paths

Prefer coherent paths, disciplines, skill trees or developmental routes over a flat catalogue of arbitrary superpowers.

A path should define:
- origin/prerequisites;
- core principle or capability family;
- entry requirements;
- stages and branches;
- skills/techniques available at each stage;
- resource and activation requirements;
- constraints, failure modes and counters;
- compatibility or incompatibility with other paths;
- training, acquisition and progression conditions;
- rarity and access routes.

The system should allow plausible expansion where the world's ontology supports it. It must not treat the current list of famous abilities as the complete ceiling of possibility.

## 5. Ability resolution

An ability description is not an execution permission. Resolve every use against:
- user/character capability and current development;
- target and range;
- line of sight or other targeting condition;
- activation time and interruption;
- cost, reserves, cooldown and strain;
- environmental interaction;
- resistance, countermeasures and competing effects;
- established world rules.

Track power resources only when they matter. Do not invent numerical costs without a defensible model. A failed activation can still consume resources if the established mechanic supports that outcome.

## 6. Item rarity and grading

Items may have rarity, quality, provenance, condition, compatibility and unique properties. Grading is an internal classification aid, not necessarily an in-world label or a guarantee of combat value. Rarity should emerge from setting logic, production, access, history and scarcity rather than arbitrary color tiers alone.

## 7. Suggestion engine

Suggestions should identify:
- useful actions and combinations;
- overlooked options;
- risks and incompatibilities;
- missing prerequisites;
- plausible opportunities;
- consequences the player may not have considered.

Suggestions are advisory. They must not become mandatory actions, secretly rewrite the character, or force a trope-driven plot direction. Avoid repetitive obvious suggestions and do not pretend an option exists when the world model does not support it.
